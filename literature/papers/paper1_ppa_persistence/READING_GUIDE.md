# Paper 1 — technical reading guide

This guide is designed for rapid reading by the project owner and for future AI-assisted literature synthesis. It complements `INDEX.md`: the index tells us **which sources matter**, while this guide and the linked reading notes explain **what each source actually shows, how it was studied, and what can safely be cited**.

## Reading-note standard

Each source note records, where the paper actually reports them:

- 60-second summary;
- exact role in Paper 1;
- design and sample;
- exposure/predictor and outcome definitions;
- statistical or qualitative method;
- key results with `n`, effect sizes, coefficients/correlations, confidence intervals, test statistics, p-values, heterogeneity, or model fit when available;
- short quotations worth remembering;
- what the source supports in the CMAT manuscript;
- what it **does not** support;
- local source path and table/page anchors for verification.

Do **not** invent a p-value, coefficient, R², sample size, or effect size when the article is conceptual, qualitative, or does not report one. In those cases the note must explicitly say that no inferential statistic applies.

## How to use this in manuscript drafting

A paper can enter Paper 1 in four different ways:

1. **theory/mechanism** — explains how student and institutional factors may interact;
2. **field/context** — defines formal mathematics support and what prior MSS work tends to evaluate;
3. **empirical comparator** — provides an effect size, qualitative pattern, or intervention result to compare with CMAT;
4. **methodological warning** — shows why observed support use cannot automatically be interpreted as treatment, motivation, engagement, or habit.

Paper 1 remains observational. These sources help formulate mechanisms and comparisons; none converts PPA exposure or CMAT attendance into an exogenous treatment.
