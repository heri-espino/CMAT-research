# Wilcox, Winn & Fyvie-Gauld (2005) — *The role of social support in the first-year experience*

## Citation

Wilcox, P., Winn, S., & Fyvie-Gauld, M. (2005). ‘It was nothing to do with the university, it was just the people’: the role of social support in the first-year experience of higher education. *Studies in Higher Education, 30*(6), 707–722. https://doi.org/10.1080/03075070500340036

## 60-second summary

This is a **qualitative first-year retention study** based on interviews with 34 students. The authors argue that retention cannot be understood only through academic integration: social integration and social support are also central. Students who stayed and students who withdrew describe friendship networks, living arrangements, tutors and other sources of support differently.

For Paper 1, the paper is valuable both substantively and strategically: it is a direct *Studies in Higher Education* precedent showing that first-year support processes and persistence can be studied without reducing them to grades alone.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** supporting first-year/retention literature.
- **Used in:** Introduction and Discussion.
- **Main job:** show that persistence-related behaviour is embedded in social and institutional relationships, not just academic need.
- **Paper 1 connection:** later CMAT use may reflect familiarity, relationships, perceived accessibility or stable help-seeking preferences; our administrative data cannot distinguish these mechanisms without survey/interview evidence.

## Design and method

- **Study type:** qualitative interview study.
- **Institution/programme:** first-year Applied Social Science students, University of Brighton.
- **Entry cohort:** 215 first-year students in the programme.
- **Interview sample:** `n = 34`.
  - stayed/completed first year: `n = 22`;
  - withdrew: `n = 12`.
- **Interview duration:** approximately 45–60 minutes.
- **Analysis:** constant comparative method from grounded theory.
- **Inferential statistics:** **none**. No regression, t-test, p-value, β or R² is the basis of the conclusions.

## Sample detail

The programme cohort was predominantly female (`80%`), white (`90%`) and under age 21 (`77%`); `16%` were age 25 or older. These percentages describe the broader first-year population, not causal covariates in a statistical model.

## What this paper lets us say

- First-year persistence is socially as well as academically situated.
- Support relationships may matter through emotional, instrumental, informational and appraisal functions.
- Qualitative work is useful for mechanisms that administrative traces cannot identify.

## What it does **not** let us say

- Social support statistically causes retention in this sample.
- CMAT use is equivalent to friendship/social integration.
- A measured numerical effect can be transferred to CMAT.
