# van Herpen et al. (2020) — *A head start in higher education*

## Citation

van Herpen, S. G. A., Meeuwisse, M., Hofman, W. H. A., & Severiens, S. E. (2020). A head start in higher education: the effect of a transition intervention on interaction, sense of belonging, and academic performance. *Studies in Higher Education, 45*(4), 862–877. https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2019.1572088

## 60-second summary

This is a **quasi-experimental first-year intervention study** in *Studies in Higher Education*. A four-day pre-academic programme was attended voluntarily by 58 incoming law students; 237 non-participants formed the control group. The study examines interaction with faculty/peers, belonging and academic performance.

Participants reported stronger formal faculty interaction, formal peer interaction and informal peer interaction; there was no significant difference in belonging. Participants also earned higher first-course grades and higher cumulative first-year GPA and were more likely to pass the first course, while first-year retention did not differ significantly.

For Paper 1, it provides a useful journal-specific comparator showing how an institutionally provided first-year experience may be associated with effects that persist across the first year, while also illustrating the selection problem created by voluntary participation.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** supporting but quantitatively important SHE precedent.
- **Used in:** literature review / Discussion / limitations.
- **Main job:** demonstrate that first-year institutional interventions can be studied with behavioural and academic outcomes over time.
- **Paper 1 connection:** like CMAT use, programme participation was voluntary, so selection remains an alternative explanation even with a quasi-experimental comparison.

## Design and sample

- **Design:** quasi-experimental, non-randomized intervention/control comparison.
- **Setting:** law school at a large Dutch state-funded university.
- **Experimental group:** `n = 58`.
- **Control group:** `n = 237`.
- **Total nominal sample:** `N = 295`, with some outcome-specific missingness.
- **Intervention:** four-day pre-academic transition programme before the academic year.
- **Academic outcomes:** first-course grade, cumulative first-year GPA, first-course pass, first-year pass/retention.
- **Psychosocial outcomes:** formal/informal interactions with peers/faculty; sense of belonging.

### Baseline comparability checks

No statistically significant group differences were reported for the main measured background variables. Example: secondary-school GPA was `6.65 (SD=.63)` for intervention participants vs `6.59 (SD=.52)` for the available control subsample, `t=-.59`, `df=118`, `p=.56`.

This does **not** remove unmeasured self-selection because students volunteered for the intervention.

## Statistical methods

- MANOVA for interaction/belonging outcomes.
- MANOVA / between-subject F tests for continuous academic outcomes.
- Chi-square tests for binary pass/retention outcomes.
- Effect sizes reported for significant intervention differences.
- Scale reliability assessed with Cronbach's alpha.

## Key quantitative results

### Interaction / belonging (Table 3)

Overall multivariate test: `F=3.95`, `df=5`, `p=.002`, partial `η²=.07`.

| Outcome | F | p | Partial η² | Adjusted R² | Effect size |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Formal faculty interaction | `6.66` | `.010` | `.02` | `.02` | `.24` |
| Informal faculty interaction | `2.63` | `.106` | `.01` | `.01` | not significant |
| Formal peer interaction | `6.70` | `.010` | `.02` | `.02` | `.25` |
| Informal peer interaction | `13.13` | `.001` | `.05` | `.04` | `.33` |
| Sense of belonging | `.25` | `.615` | `.00` | `-.01` | not significant |

### Academic performance (Table 4)

Overall multivariate academic-performance test: `F=47.71`, `df=2`, `p=.001`.

- **First-course grade:** intervention mean `6.36` vs control `5.69` (1–10 scale); `F=15.03`, `df=1`, `p=.001`, effect size `=.28`.
- **First-year cumulative GPA:** intervention mean `6.44` vs control `6.07`; `F=5.26`, `df=1`, `p=.023`, effect size `=.36`.

### Passing outcomes (Table 5)

- **First-course pass:** `χ²=7.46`, `df=1`, `p=.006`; intervention participants had a higher pass probability.
- **First-year pass/retention:** `χ²=2.94`, `p=.086`; not statistically significant at `.05`.

## Measurement reliability worth noting

Examples reported by group include:

- informal peer interaction: `α_exp=.71`, `α_control=.81`;
- sense of belonging: `α_exp=.82`, `α_control=.84`.

The authors note weaker reliability for some interaction scales in the relatively small experimental group.

Conclusion/discussion anchor.

## What this paper lets us say

- Institutional first-year interventions can be associated with later academic outcomes, not only immediate perceptions.
- A null result on belonging can coexist with positive interaction and grade results.
- *Studies in Higher Education* accepts careful quasi-experimental work on first-year interventions when mechanisms and limitations are explicit.

## What it does **not** let us say

- Voluntary intervention participation is equivalent to random assignment.
- The reported differences are free of unmeasured self-selection.
- CMAT/PPA should have the same effect size.
- Our later CMAT use is caused by the PPA incentive.
