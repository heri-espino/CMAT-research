# Fong et al. (2023) — *Academic Help-Seeking and Achievement of Postsecondary Students*

## Citation

Fong, C. J., Gonzales, C., Hill-Troglin Cox, C., & Shinn, H. B. (2023). Academic help-seeking and achievement of postsecondary students: A meta-analytic investigation. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 115*(1), 1–21. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000725

## 60-second summary

This is the strongest broad quantitative anchor in the current Paper 1 library for the relationship between **academic help-seeking and achievement in postsecondary education**. It meta-analyzes 108 studies (119 samples; total `N = 37,941`) and shows that treating all help-seeking as one construct hides important differences. General help-seeking is only weakly positively associated with achievement, while **formal** and **instrumental** help-seeking are positive and avoidant/executive forms are negative.

The closest construct to CMAT is **formal help-seeking**, because the authors code instructors and academic support centres as formal sources. However, a CMAT visit count does not reveal whether the student is seeking instrumental help, executive help, or something else.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** core empirical/meta-analytic anchor.
- **Used in:** Introduction, literature review, Discussion.
- **Main job:** establish that academic help-seeking is heterogeneous and that formal support-seeking can be positively associated with achievement.
- **Paper 1 connection:** CMAT is formal institutional help-seeking; persistent CMAT use can therefore be discussed as persistence in a formal support behaviour, not automatically as persistence in motivation or “good” help-seeking quality.

## Design and method

- **Study type:** meta-analysis.
- **Included evidence:** 108 studies, 119 samples, `N = 37,941` postsecondary students.
- **Primary effect metric:** Pearson correlation `r` between help-seeking variables and achievement.
- **Weighting:** inverse-variance weighting.
- **Dependency handling:** multivariate meta-analytic models with **robust variance estimation (RVE)** for dependent effect sizes.
- **Uncertainty:** 95% confidence intervals and 95% prediction intervals.
- **Heterogeneity:** Cochran's `Q`, `I²`, study-level and effect-level variance components.
- **Moderators:** meta-regression.
- **Publication bias:** publication-status moderator plus Egger regression tests adapted to the multivariate/RVE framework.

### Moderator result worth remembering

For instrumental help-seeking, the country moderator was statistically significant in meta-regression (`β = .30`, `SE = .08`, 95% CI `[.15, .46]`, `p < .05`). The authors report larger positive correlations in the small set of non-U.S. studies (`r = .40`, `SE = .08`, `p = .041`, `k = 3`) than in U.S.-based samples (`r = .08`, `SE = .02`, `p = .004`, `k = 21`). This should be treated cautiously because only three non-U.S. studies contributed to that comparison.

### Heterogeneity / publication-bias message

Nearly all main `Q` tests were significant and most `I²` values exceeded 75%, indicating substantial heterogeneity. That matters for CMAT: the relationship between help-seeking and performance depends strongly on how help-seeking is defined and on context.

Egger-regression confidence intervals reported in Table 4 included zero across the help-seeking categories, so the paper did not find clear evidence from those tests of funnel-plot asymmetry/publication bias.

## Short quotation worth remembering

> “the quality of the help-seeking matters”

Discussion anchor: around Table 2 / overall meta-analytic findings.

## What this paper lets us say

- Academic help-seeking is not a unitary behaviour.
- Formal help-seeking, the category closest to CMAT, shows a small positive pooled association with achievement (`r=.12`).
- Avoiding help when needed is negatively associated with achievement.
- High heterogeneity means context and operational definition matter.

## What it does **not** let us say

- A CMAT visit is necessarily instrumental/adaptive help-seeking.
- More CMAT visits must monotonically improve grades.
- Help-seeking causes achievement; the meta-analysis is dominated by correlational primary studies.
- Persistence in CMAT use proves habit formation.
