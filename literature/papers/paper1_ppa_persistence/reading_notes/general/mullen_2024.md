# Mullen, Howard & Cronin (2024) — *A scoping literature review of the impact and evaluation of mathematics and statistics support in higher education*

## Citation

Mullen, C., Howard, E., & Cronin, A. (2024). A scoping literature review of the impact and evaluation of mathematics and statistics support in higher education. *Educational Studies in Mathematics, 117*, 1–22. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10649-024-10332-6

## 60-second summary

This is the most up-to-date broad map in our library of how **mathematics and statistics support (MSS)** is evaluated in higher education. It is important less because it gives one pooled effect and more because it shows how heterogeneous and methodologically uneven the MSS literature is.

The review includes 148 publications representing 136 evaluation studies from 12 countries. Most studies use quantitative data, but designs and analyses vary widely. The review reports that many studies find positive MSS outcomes, while also emphasizing selection bias, weak reporting and lack of rigorous designs.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** core field/context source.
- **Used in:** Introduction, description of CMAT as MSS, Discussion/limitations.
- **Main job:** show that CMAT belongs to a mature but methodologically heterogeneous MSS literature.
- **Paper 1 connection:** persistence of support use is less commonly studied than immediate outcomes; our longitudinal MU→Calculus linkage addresses a gap in a field dominated by cross-sectional/service-evaluation studies.

## Design and method

- **Study type:** systematic scoping review.
- **Protocol:** predefined protocol based on PRISMA guidance for scoping reviews.
- **Search coverage:** five databases, proceedings of eight conferences, two earlier MSS review reference lists, six MSS/mathematics-education networks/websites, plus relevant journal/other sources.
- **Coverage period:** publications from 2000 through November 2022.
- **Screening:** two rounds; multi-author screening with disagreement resolution.
- **Included:** 148 publications representing 136 MSS evaluation studies, from 12 countries.
- **Primary synthesis:** descriptive mapping of provision type, data source, data type, analysis method, reported impact and potential bias.
- **Meta-analysis:** **none**; there is no single pooled β, R² or average causal effect.

## Key methodological message

The review's most useful point for CMAT is that apparently positive MSS findings come from a literature with considerable heterogeneity in exposure definitions, outcomes and statistical methods. User/non-user comparisons are frequent, but self-selection into support is a recurring threat.

This makes our insistence on observational language, explicit cohort definitions and sensitivity analyses important rather than cosmetic.

## Short quotation worth remembering

> “The majority of the broad range of results ... show a positive impact on students, but not all did.”

Use as a field-level summary, not as proof that CMAT causes positive outcomes.

## What this paper lets us say

- CMAT fits a recognized definition of organized mathematics/statistics support outside normal teaching.
- MSS evaluation commonly uses user/non-user comparisons, t-tests, chi-square, ANOVA and regression, but designs vary widely.
- Positive grade/pass/confidence findings are common in the literature.
- Rigorous study design and bias control remain important gaps.

## What it does **not** let us say

- The 18 positive grade studies imply an average causal MSS effect.
- Every MSS centre is beneficial.
- CMAT's effect size should match any pooled value — this review does not estimate one.
