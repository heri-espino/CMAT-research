# Paper 1 literature index — PPA persistence

This index is intentionally selective. It identifies the literature that most directly supports the persistence-after-incentive manuscript.

## Core theoretical / framing literature

### `gneezy_2011_when-incentives-dont-work`
- Priority: **core**
- Role: incentive mechanisms, crowd-out, and what may happen when incentives are removed.
- Used in: Introduction, Discussion.
- Do not claim: that CMAT/PPA is experimentally identified or that crowd-out is directly observed in CMAT data.

### `kahu_2013_student-engagement-framework`
- Priority: **core**
- Role: student engagement framework and mechanisms linking institutional context with student engagement.
- Used in: Introduction, conceptual framing, Discussion.

### `kahu_2018_student-engagement-educational-interface`
- Priority: **core**
- Role: educational-interface model; useful for positioning support use as an interaction between student characteristics and institutional conditions.
- Used in: conceptual framing and Discussion.

### `fong_2023_academic-help-seeking-achievement`
- Priority: **core**
- Role: meta-analytic anchor for academic help-seeking and achievement.
- Used in: Introduction and interpretation of persistent support-seeking.
- Do not claim: that visit counts reveal motivation or habit.

## Competing explanations that must remain visible

Paper 1 should distinguish at least three explanations for later CMAT use:

1. threshold-limited/incentive-linked use;
2. persistence/familiarization with formal support after initial exposure;
3. stable selection (need, organization, prior preferences, academic characteristics) that drives both MU and later Calculus use.

The literature review should not present the observed association as sufficient to choose among these mechanisms causally.

## Secondary institutional framing

- `hanushek_2008_cognitive-skills-economic-development` — use only for the broad institutional chain `provision/arrangements → incentive structures → actor behaviour`; not evidence that PPA causes persistence, habits, motivation, or academic gains.

## Peripheral analogies

- `charness_2009_incentives-exercise`

These may inform incentive persistence conceptually but should not carry the educational claim.

## Interpretation guardrails

- Never say PPA causally created CMAT use or persistence.
- Never infer habit, intrinsic motivation, or psychological state from visit records.
- Prefer `institutional participation incentive`, `formal academic help-seeking`, `behavioural persistence`, and `pattern consistent with threshold-limited use`.
- The piecewise visit-count model is descriptive, not a regression-discontinuity design.
