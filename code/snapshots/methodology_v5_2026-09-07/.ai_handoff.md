## Non-negotiable statistical definitions

- Primary contemporaneous cohort: **6,627** first real MU attempts in terms with CMAT coverage.
- Classroom: professor × course × academic period.
- Official degree programme comes from the academic record linked by student ID; do not substitute self-reported Form career as the baseline programme variable.
- Primary continuous outcome: classroom-relative `Z_GRADE_PRIMARY` after adverse BV/RT/BA grades are imputed from the **within-classroom observed numeric distribution below 7.5** using `scipy.stats.gaussian_kde(..., bw_method=None)` (SciPy default Scott factor). Uniform `U(0,7.5)` is used only when the classroom has no observed sub-7.5 numeric grade; a degenerate observed pool is resampled empirically.
- Primary visit groups for performance: **0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4+**. Do not default back to only `>3` vs `<=3`.
- Longitudinal means the same student is linked from an earlier MU observation to a later Calculus observation. Temporal ordering is not causal identification.
- The **4,211** future-progressor subset is valid for longitudinal/progressor questions but conditions on a future event. It is a sensitivity population for MU performance, not a silent replacement of N=6,627.

## Current key results

### Exact groups in all first-MU (N=6,627)
Mean classroom Z: 0=`-0.0608`; 1=`0.2189`; 2=`0.2466`; 3=`0.2778`; 4+=`0.3549`.
Welch: `F(4,594.83)=43.46`, `p=5.23e-32`; Brown-Forsythe `p=6.07e-13`.
All four 0-vs-positive Games-Howell comparisons are significant. No positive-vs-positive comparison is significant. In the classroom-FE + official-career model with classroom-clustered SE, all four 0-vs-positive contrasts survive Holm; none of the six positive-vs-positive contrasts survive Holm.

### Exact counts 0-12
Sparse tail at 8-12 (`n=19,11,13,2,4`). Among users 1-12, Spearman rho≈0.041, p≈0.155. Cluster-robust linear slope ≈0.0235 Z/visit, p≈0.030, but stable 1-7 slope≈0.0146, p≈0.421. Treat full-tail significance as fragile; there is no robust monotonic dose-response among positive users.

### Temporal recurrence
Peak detection: daily unique-student counts, zero-filled, centered 7-day rolling sum, `scipy.signal.find_peaks`, minimum distance 21 days, prominence `max(5,0.12*max rolling)`, snapped to busiest observed day within ±3 days.
- All CMAT: median 5 peaks/term; median gap 30.5 days; 73.7% of gaps in 24-38 days.
- First-MU during MU term: median 3 peaks; median gap 31.0 days; 60.9% in 24-38 days.
ACF after weekday mean removal + linear detrend peaks at lag 30 days in both populations (r≈0.210 and 0.216). Periodogram median dominant periods ≈27.0 and 25.8 days. Descriptive recurrence only; do not label peaks as exams without calendars.

### Career analyses
Use official career from academic rows. Minimum n=30 is only an inference/reporting filter; no career is deleted from data.
- Broad covered math attempts: 82 codes total; 44 n>=30.
- First-MU: 77 codes total; 29 n>=30, N=6,374; 48 small codes sum 253 students and are listed in report appendix.
- Linked later-Calculus risk set: 51 codes; 24 n>=30.
Career-level use-vs-mean-Z relation is ecological. First-MU has no clear association (Pearson r=.115, p=.554; Spearman rho=.261, p=.172).

### Future-progressor sensitivity N=4,211
MU means are all positive (0=.315, 1=.429, 2=.459, 3=.443, 4+=.483), showing strong selection of future progressors. Welch remains significant, but positive-positive contrasts are not. Use this to explain why future conditioning changes the estimand.
