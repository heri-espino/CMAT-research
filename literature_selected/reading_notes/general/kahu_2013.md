# Kahu (2013) — *Framing student engagement in higher education*

## Citation

Kahu, E. R. (2013). Framing student engagement in higher education. *Studies in Higher Education, 38*(5), 758–773. https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2011.598505

## 60-second summary

This is a **conceptual review and framework paper**, not an empirical treatment-effect study. Kahu reviews four dominant perspectives on student engagement — behavioural, psychological, sociocultural and holistic — and argues that the field frequently confuses three distinct things: **antecedents of engagement, the state of engagement itself, and consequences of engagement**. The proposed framework places engagement between student/institutional influences and later outcomes.

For CMAT, this distinction is crucial: PPA rules and CMAT availability are institutional/contextual influences; a recorded CMAT visit is an observed behaviour; neither directly measures the underlying psychological state of engagement, motivation, or habit.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** core theoretical anchor.
- **Used in:** Introduction, conceptual framework, Discussion.
- **Main job:** prevent us from equating observed CMAT attendance with latent engagement or motivation.
- **Paper 1 connection:** the incentive-linked first-year environment can be treated as part of the institutional context that may shape behaviour; later CMAT use is an observed outcome that may be consistent with persistence/familiarization but is not proof of a psychological mechanism.

## What this paper lets us say

- Student behaviour should be interpreted in relation to both student characteristics and institutional context.
- A first-year institutional incentive may be one contextual influence among many.
- Longitudinal observation is valuable because engagement/behaviour can vary over time and across contexts.

## What it does **not** let us say

- CMAT attendance = engagement.
- Three visits = motivation, habit, self-efficacy or belonging.
- PPA causally created persistent support-seeking.
- Any specific effect size for CMAT or academic support.
