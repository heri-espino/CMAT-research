\section{Discussion}

The distinction between initial attendance and repeated attendance is central to interpreting routine mathematics-support records. The strongest result is the higher adjusted final performance and PASS probability of students with at least one recorded CMAT visit compared with those with none. This broad separation persists after controlling for instructor--period and degree programme, although the analysis is observational and cannot identify what would have happened if a non-attendee had been assigned to visit the centre.

The more demanding question is whether there is a reliable progression in outcomes from one positive visit frequency to the next. Under the positive-frequency grouping selected from outcome-blind support, the omnibus tests and every Holm-adjusted pairwise comparison were inconclusive. This does not establish equality or prove that additional support cannot matter; the high-frequency cells are sparse, the available information does not include pre-visit skill or tutoring duration, and the correction explicitly protects against selecting the most favourable result from many comparisons. Most importantly, the absence of multiplicity-supported \emph{adjacent} contrasts is inconsistent with an empirically established staircase of improvements after successive visits.

There are nevertheless reasons not to conclude that all positive frequencies have identical associations. In the more detailed, zero-inclusive model, the seven-or-more-visits category had a higher adjusted PASS probability than the one-visit category after Holm correction; with instructor-level clustering in the originally grouped user-only model, the corresponding broader $6+$ versus one-visit PASS difference was also significant. The different conclusions arise from specifications with different group support, samples, and covariance assumptions, and the fine-grained upper tail was not the grouping originally frozen before outcome inspection. The appropriate interpretation is therefore that \emph{broader low-versus-high differences remain plausible}, whereas the available contrasts do not justify selecting a sequence of precise attendance changepoints.

The PPA1 participation rule is central to the interpretation of visit frequency. Under the documented arrangement, PPA1 points were earned upon completing three CMAT visits. The four descriptive bands, $0$, $1$--$3$, $4$--$6$, and $7+$, accordingly separate non-use, use up to completion of the incentive condition, subsequent use and the upper tail. The $3|4$ display boundary marks visits beyond PPA1 completion; $2|3$ marks reaching completion, and neither comparison constitutes a causal discontinuity test. The bands are a hypothesised institutional interpretation, not proof of four distinct statistical populations or homogeneous effects within each band. Visits beyond three may reflect sustained help-seeking, continuing difficulty or other unobserved conditions.

The descriptive composition of non-passing outcomes suggests an additional research question. Students who attended CMAT and nevertheless did not pass had a greater proportion of BV/RT/BA records instead of numeric grades below 7.5 than non-attendees who did not pass. A previously fitted ancillary instructor--period-adjusted comparison of administrative versus numeric non-PASS outcomes estimated a 13.2-percentage-point difference (95\% CI 6.8--19.6; nominal $p<0.001$). This analysis is \emph{exploratory}, conditional on non-PASS and outside the article's prespecified Holm families. Scholarship retention offers one possible explanation because institutional scholarships can require a minimum cumulative average; however, withdrawal limits also differ by scholarship scheme, and the available records do not identify scholarship recipients, reasons for withdrawing, or whether an individual BV, RT or BA actually protected academic standing. It would be incorrect to label all administrative outcomes as deliberate grade avoidance or as evidence that students exhausted academic effort before withdrawing.

This work contributes to evaluation practices in mathematics-support settings by distinguishing an initial-use contrast from a multiplicity-adjusted investigation of positive frequencies. The use of instructor--period contextual adjustment, outcome-blind support criteria for upper-tail pooling, and transparent Holm families makes the inferential limits clearer than a plot of unadjusted means or selectively highlighted $p$-values. Such care is especially important for institutions that hope to assess support effectiveness from administrative records without observing the reasons for attendance.

\section{Directions for future research}

Two institutional mechanisms deserve dedicated evaluation beyond the present observational Holm comparison. First, the relationship between mathematics-support attendance, scholarship eligibility and the administrative resolution of a non-passing course should be studied using authorised, de-identified information on scholarship type, prior cumulative average, the applicable historical policy on failed grades and withdrawal counts, timing of CMAT visits and withdrawals, and, where ethically and practically feasible, direct evidence of students' reasons for their decisions. A low numeric grade may threaten a minimum average, but an administrative withdrawal may itself count against scholarship conditions; therefore grade-protection behaviour remains a hypothesis to test, not a finding of this paper.

Second, the PPA1 participation incentive that could be completed with three CMAT visits motivates a study of whether the credit requirement changes first attendance, the probability of completing three visits, and attendance beyond that requirement. A future design would require independently verified historic PPA1 rules and eligibility by cohort, along with visit dates and an appropriate comparison population or well-defined policy change. Descriptive changes around the third and fourth visits do not themselves identify an incentive effect.

\section{Limitations}

The primary concern is self-selection. Students are not randomly assigned to CMAT or to a particular visit count; academic need, existing mathematical preparation, motivation, schedules and instructor encouragement may influence both attendance and final performance. Adjustment for instructor--period and degree programme does not account for every relevant baseline characteristic. A sufficiently complete, comparable university entrance examination measure would permit an additional observed-preparation sensitivity but not, by itself, a causal interpretation.

Attendance and final outcomes are recorded within the same period. Students may visit because they encounter difficulties, return when support seems useful, or stop accumulating visits because they withdraw before the end of the period. The attendance records do not document duration or instructional content, so visit count cannot be equated with the delivered quantity of mathematical instruction. The historical timing and universality of the PPA option also require independent institutional confirmation.

Upper-frequency cells are small, especially exact six visits and the pooled $7+$ tail, reducing within-context overlap and precision. The pooled-$6+$ specification was selected with a support audit before reviewing outcomes, but the more detailed $7+$ contrast was examined subsequently; the distinction limits its confirmatory interpretation. The observed instructor-level clustering sensitivity also shows that inferential conclusions for widely separated visit groups can depend on how repeated instructor contributions are treated.

Finally, the standardised continuous outcome relies on a documented numerical representation of administrative results, whereas PASS does not, and the complete-case numeric sensitivity conditions on receiving a final numeric grade. The two main outcomes consequently have distinct interpretation and measurement assumptions. Holm controls family-wise false rejections within the declared pairwise family when its tests are calibrated, but does not correct unmeasured confounding, specification choices or broader research-wide exploration; non-rejection is not proof of equivalence.

\section{Conclusion}

Among 6,627 first eligible university mathematics-course attempts, the PPA1 three-visit completion condition makes the descriptive distinction between $1$--$3$ and $4+$ meaningful, but the hypothesis of separate $0$, $1$--$3$, $4$--$6$, and $7+$ regimes remains unproven. Recorded CMAT use was associated with higher final standardised grades and a greater adjusted probability of passing than zero use. However, the originally support-based comparisons among positive attendance frequencies yielded no Holm-significant pairwise contrasts, and no successive-visit gradient was reliably resolved. A higher PASS probability for very frequent users emerged in exploratory full-grid or alternative clustering specifications, but its sensitivity to grouping and inferential population prevents treating a particular visit count as an established academic threshold. The results support distinguishing initial engagement from frequency among attendees when evaluating mathematics-support services, while leaving causal effects of attendance undetermined.
