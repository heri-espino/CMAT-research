We then fit linear regression models with fixed effects for class and indicators for degree programme, with standard errors clustered by class. The main model includes separate indicators for 1, 2, 3, and 4 or more visits, using students with no recorded visits as the reference group. We also report all ten pairwise comparisons among the five attendance groups and adjust the corresponding $p$-values using the Holm procedure. The regression coefficients describe adjusted within-class associations and are not interpreted as causal effects of mathematics support.

\section{Results}

\subsection{CMAT attendance during the first MU attempt}

Among the 6,627 students in the study sample, 5,393 had no recorded CMAT visit during the MU academic period and 1,234 (18.6\%) attended at least once. Among students who attended, 517 made one visit, 245 made two visits, 170 made three visits, and 302 made four or more visits. \Cref{fig:visits} shows the full distribution of recorded attendance. Most students did not attend CMAT during the period, and higher visit counts were progressively less common.

\subsection{Standardised grades by attendance group}

\Cref{tab:means} and \Cref{fig:means} show higher mean within-class standardised grades among students who attended CMAT than among those with no recorded visits. Mean $Z$ was $-0.061$ for students with no visits and $0.219$, $0.247$, $0.278$, and $0.355$ for students with 1, 2, 3, and 4 or more visits, respectively. The means increase with attendance frequency in the main analysis; the pairwise comparisons show whether the four groups of attendees differ clearly from one another.

The Brown--Forsythe test indicated unequal variances across the five groups ($F=15.88$, $p<0.001$), and Welch's ANOVA showed an overall difference in group means, $F(4,594.83)=43.46$, $p<0.001$. Games--Howell post hoc comparisons showed that students in each of the four attendance groups had a significantly higher mean $Z$ than students with no recorded visits, with adjusted $p\leq0.000002$ in all four comparisons. By contrast, none of the six comparisons among students who attended different numbers of times was significant at the 5\% level: adjusted $p$-values ranged from $0.090$ for one versus four-or-more visits to $0.994$ for two versus three visits.

The clearest difference is therefore between students who attended CMAT and those who did not. Although the mean standardised grade increased across the five attendance groups, the differences among students who attended one, two, three, or at least four times were not statistically significant.

\subsection{Adjusted regression results}

The regression models gave the same overall pattern. Compared with students with no recorded visits in the same class, the adjusted mean $Z$ score was $0.305$ standard deviations higher for students with one visit, $0.359$ higher for those with two visits, $0.373$ higher for those with three visits, and $0.461$ higher for those with four or more visits (\Cref{fig:adjusted}). The 95\% confidence intervals were $[0.221,0.388]$, $[0.262,0.457]$, $[0.248,0.498]$, and $[0.350,0.572]$, respectively. All four coefficients remained statistically significant after Holm adjustment across the ten pairwise comparisons.

Differences among the four groups of attendees were again less clear. None of the six pairwise comparisons among these groups remained significant after Holm adjustment. The largest adjusted difference among attendees was between one visit and four or more visits ($0.156$ standard deviations); the unadjusted $p$-value was $0.025$, but the Holm-adjusted value was $0.153$. Thus, the adjusted analysis provides stronger evidence of a difference between students who attended and those who did not than of systematic differences according to attendance frequency among students who used CMAT.

Excluding smaller classes produced very similar estimates. The coefficients for one, two, three, and four-or-more visits were $0.305Z$, $0.359Z$, $0.373Z$, and $0.461Z$ in the full sample. Restricting the analysis to classes with at least 20 students retained 6,255 students across 165 classes and yielded corresponding estimates of $0.312Z$, $0.367Z$, $0.384Z$, and $0.457Z$.

\subsection{Sensitivity analyses}

The comparison between attendees and non-attendees was also retained when the non-numeric course outcomes were handled differently. \Cref{fig:sensitivity} compares mean standardised grades by attendance group under the primary outcome definition, the uniform below-pass imputation, and the complete-case analysis restricted to numeric final grades. In all three analyses, students with no recorded visits had a negative mean $Z$ score and each group of students who attended CMAT had a positive mean. However, mean performance did not increase consistently with attendance frequency across all three analyses. In the numeric complete-case analysis, for example, the means for 1, 2, 3, and 4+ visits were approximately $0.256$, $0.193$, $0.326$, and $0.200$, respectively. Across the sensitivity analyses, the most consistent result is therefore the difference between students who attended CMAT and those who did not; evidence of progressively higher performance with more visits is less stable.
