We then fit ordinary least-squares models with class fixed effects, indicators for degree programme, and standard errors clustered by class. The main model includes indicators for 1, 2, 3, and 4 or more visits, with students who recorded no visits as the reference group. We also report all ten pairwise comparisons among the five attendance groups and adjust the corresponding $p$-values using the Holm procedure. The coefficients describe within-class associations between attendance and performance and should not be interpreted as causal effects of mathematics support.

\section{Results}

\subsection{CMAT attendance in first-attempt MU}

Among the 6,627 students in the primary cohort, 5,393 had no recorded CMAT visit during the MU academic period and 1,234 (18.6\%) attended at least once. Among students who attended, 517 made one visit, 245 made two visits, 170 made three visits, and 302 made four or more visits. \Cref{fig:visits} shows the full distribution of recorded attendance. Most students did not attend CMAT during the period, and higher visit counts were progressively less common.

\subsection{Classroom-relative performance by attendance group}

\Cref{tab:means} and \Cref{fig:means} show higher mean classroom-relative performance among students who attended CMAT than among those with no recorded visits. Mean $Z$ was $-0.061$ for students with no visits and $0.219$, $0.247$, $0.278$, and $0.355$ for students with 1, 2, 3, and 4 or more visits, respectively. The means increase with attendance frequency in the primary specification, but pairwise comparisons are needed before interpreting this ordering as evidence that students who attended more often also performed progressively better.

The Brown--Forsythe test indicated unequal variances across the five groups ($F=15.88$, $p<0.001$), and Welch's ANOVA showed an overall difference in group means, $F(4,594.83)=43.46$, $p<0.001$. Games--Howell post hoc comparisons showed that each group of students who attended CMAT had significantly higher mean $Z$ than students with no recorded visits, with adjusted $p\leq0.000002$ in all four comparisons. By contrast, none of the six comparisons among students who attended different numbers of times was significant at the 5\% level: adjusted $p$-values ranged from $0.090$ for one versus four-or-more visits to $0.994$ for two versus three visits.

The clearest statistical distinction is therefore between students who attended CMAT and those who did not. Although the mean $Z$ score increased across the five attendance groups in the primary specification, the differences among students who attended one, two, three, or at least four times were not statistically significant.

\subsection{Within-class associations}

Models with class fixed effects and adjustment for degree programme gave the same overall pattern. Compared with students with no recorded visits in the same class, the estimated differences were $0.305$ standard deviations for one visit, $0.359$ for two visits, $0.373$ for three visits, and $0.461$ for four or more visits (\Cref{fig:adjusted}). The corresponding 95\% confidence intervals were $[0.221,0.388]$, $[0.262,0.457]$, $[0.248,0.498]$, and $[0.350,0.572]$, respectively. All four coefficients remained statistically significant after the prespecified Holm adjustment across the ten pairwise comparisons.

Differences among the four groups of students who attended CMAT were again less clear. None of the six pairwise comparisons among these groups remained significant after Holm adjustment. The largest adjusted difference was between one visit and four-or-more visits ($0.156Z$ in favour of the latter); the unadjusted $p$-value was $0.025$, but the Holm-adjusted value was $0.153$. Thus, the adjusted analysis provides stronger evidence of a difference between attendance and non-attendance than of differences associated with the frequency of attendance among students who used CMAT.

A sensitivity analysis excluding smaller classes produced very similar estimates. The primary coefficients for one, two, three, and four-or-more visits were $0.305Z$, $0.359Z$, $0.373Z$, and $0.461Z$, respectively. Restricting the analysis to classes with at least 20 students retained 6,255 students across 165 classes and yielded corresponding estimates of $0.312Z$, $0.367Z$, $0.384Z$, and $0.457Z$.

\subsection{Sensitivity to outcome construction}

The main result was also retained under alternative treatments of non-numeric adverse course outcomes. \Cref{fig:sensitivity} compares mean $Z$ scores by attendance group under the primary outcome construction, a sensitivity analysis using a common imputed value for adverse non-numeric outcomes, and a complete-case analysis restricted to numeric final grades. In all three specifications, students with no recorded visits had a negative mean classroom-relative score and each group of students who attended CMAT had a positive mean. However, the ordering of the attendance groups was not consistently monotonic. In the numeric complete-case analysis, for example, the means for 1, 2, 3, and 4+ visits were approximately $0.256$, $0.193$, $0.326$, and $0.200$, respectively. The result that is most consistent across outcome definitions is therefore the difference between students who attended CMAT and those who did not, rather than a progressively stronger association with increasing attendance frequency.
