\section{Results}

\subsection{Attendance distribution and unadjusted outcomes}

Of the 6,627 first eligible MU attempts, 5,393 students (81.4\%) had no recorded CMAT attendance and 1,234 (18.6\%) had at least one recorded visit. Positive attendance was concentrated at low frequencies: 517 students made one visit, 245 made two, 170 made three, 96 made four, 55 made five, 50 made exactly six and 101 made seven or more. \Cref{tab:visits-descriptive} presents descriptive final performance and PASS rates for the fine-resolution reporting grid. These are unadjusted summaries, not estimated changes following individual support sessions.

The descriptive means do not increase uniformly with each visit count: the exact-six mean, for instance, is lower than the five-visit mean and has a wide interval. The $7+$ cell remains small compared with the zero-visit group. These support differences are relevant when interpreting both local contrasts and non-rejection after multiple-testing correction.

\subsection{Benchmark: any use versus no recorded use}

After adjustment for instructor--period and degree programme, any recorded attendance was associated with $0.361$ SD higher final standardised performance (95\% CI $0.302$--$0.421$, $p<0.001$) and a 15.3 percentage-point higher PASS probability (95\% CI 12.4--18.1 points, $p<0.001$). This is the most precisely estimated separation in the study, but is an observational comparison of final outcomes and should not be construed as pre-to-post improvement from centre use.

\subsection{Primary frequency inference among CMAT users}

The originally selected positive-frequency grouping consists of exact 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 visits and pooled $6+$ visits, for a total of six levels. The fixed-effect omnibus test among the 1,234 users did not reject equal adjusted outcomes across these levels for continuous standardised performance ($p=0.317$) or PASS ($p=0.152$). Moreover, none of the 15 pairwise frequency contrasts per outcome remained significant after Holm adjustment.

The importance of prespecified multiplicity treatment is evident for the widest low-versus-high comparison. Between one visit and $6+$ visits, the unadjusted pairwise $p$-values were 0.022 for the standardised outcome and 0.011 for PASS, but became 0.328 and 0.171 after Holm adjustment, respectively. Thus, isolated unadjusted comparisons provide insufficient evidence for differentiated positive-frequency categories under this primary specification.

\subsection{Full eight-category profile and exploratory upper-tail resolution}

In the zero-inclusive grid $0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7+$, the adjusted model of the continuous outcome rejected equality across all levels ($p<0.001$). Every adjusted contrast between zero attendance and a positive visit category survived Holm adjustment across the 28 pairs; the weakest such comparison involved exactly six visits (adjusted $p=0.042$). All seven zero-versus-positive PASS contrasts also survived Holm correction in the corresponding full-grid family.

A notable non-adjacent PASS comparison separated the $7+$ group from one-visit users by **14.5 percentage points** (95\% Wald CI 5.9--23.1 percentage points, Holm-adjusted $p=0.0206$). This estimate comes from the full sample of 6,627 and the 28-comparison family, not the primary user-only model; the finer split of the upper tail was made after the original support audit. The result therefore identifies an interesting exploratory high-frequency profile rather than a preselected attendance threshold.

Within the seven positive-frequency categories, separate user-only omnibus tests remained inconclusive ($p=0.213$ for standardised grades and $p=0.102$ for PASS), and no positive-frequency pair survived the corresponding Holm adjustment. These findings are not numerically interchangeable with the full zero-inclusive model because removing zero-attendance students changes the sample, nuisance fit and covariance estimates in addition to the comparison family.

No adjusted adjacent visit contrast among users survived multiplicity correction in the reported analyses. In particular, exact two and three visits did not show an abrupt change at the institutionally salient participation-credit threshold. Descriptively the mean $Z$ changed from 0.267 to 0.382 between four and five visits and from 0.288 to 0.457 between exact six and $7+$, but the corresponding adjacent adjusted contrasts did not establish a break.

\subsection{Sensitivity to upper-tail grouping and level of clustering}

The pooled-$6+$ primary specification remained inconclusive among users after Holm correction, whereas the full eight-group model identified the exploratory $7+$ versus one-visit PASS comparison described above. This difference should be retained in interpretation rather than presented as if a uniquely optimal upper-tail resolution had been established.

Because the same instructor can teach in more than one period, we re-estimated uncertainty by clustering at instructor level. The any-use benchmark remained $0.361$ SD (95\% CI 0.305--0.417) and 15.3 PASS percentage points (95\% CI 12.8--17.8). In the user-only pooled-$6+$ model, the instructor-cluster omnibus test yielded $p=0.0477$ for grades and $p=0.0551$ for PASS. None of the grade contrasts survived Holm correction, but the non-adjacent PASS contrast comparing $6+$ visits with one visit was +12.0 percentage points (95\% CI 4.3--19.8; adjusted $p=0.0358$). No adjacent PASS contrast was significant. This sensitivity allows a wider low-versus-high distinction in PASS, but does not support a monotonic visit-by-visit gradient.

\subsection{Observed-numeric outcome check}

The complete-case analysis that excludes BV, RT and BA changes the analytic population and addresses a different endpoint. Across positive visit frequencies its omnibus test for the numeric-only standardised grade did not reject equality ($p=0.397$). This check does not remove concerns about selection into an observed final numeric grade and cannot establish that the imputation rule is consequence-free; it supports reporting the distinct PASS outcome alongside the standardised final-outcome measure.
