We then fitted linear regression models with instructor--period fixed effects and degree-programme indicators, with standard errors clustered at the instructor--period level. The main model included separate indicators for 1, 2, 3, and 4 or more visits, with no recorded visits as the reference category. We also calculated all ten pairwise comparisons among the five attendance groups and adjusted the corresponding $p$-values using the Holm procedure.

\section{Results}

\subsection{CMAT attendance during the first MU attempt}

Among the 6,627 students in the study sample, 5,393 had no recorded CMAT visit during the MU academic period and 1,234 (18.6\%) attended at least once. Of those who attended, 517 made one visit, 245 made two visits, 170 made three visits, and 302 made four or more visits. \Cref{fig:visits} shows the full distribution of recorded attendance. Most students did not attend CMAT during the period, and higher visit counts were progressively less common.

\subsection{Standardised grades by attendance group}

\Cref{tab:means} and \Cref{fig:means} show higher mean standardised grades among students who attended CMAT than among those with no recorded visits. Mean $Z$ was $-0.061$ for students with no visits and $0.219$, $0.247$, $0.278$, and $0.355$ for students with 1, 2, 3, and 4 or more visits, respectively. The mean standardised grade increased across the five attendance groups in the main analysis.

The Brown--Forsythe test indicated unequal variances across the five groups ($F=15.88$, $p<0.001$), and Welch's ANOVA showed an overall difference in group means, $F(4,594.83)=43.46$, $p<0.001$. Games--Howell post hoc comparisons showed that each of the four groups of students who attended CMAT had a higher mean $Z$ than students with no recorded visits (all $p<0.001$). By contrast, none of the six comparisons among students who attended different numbers of times was significant at the 5\% level; $p$-values ranged from $0.090$ for one versus four or more visits to $0.994$ for two versus three visits.

The descriptive increase in mean grade was therefore not accompanied by statistically significant differences among students who attended at different frequencies.

\subsection{Adjusted regression results}

Compared with students with no recorded visits in the same instructor--period group, the estimated differences in $Z$ were $0.305$ standard deviations for one visit, $0.359$ for two visits, $0.373$ for three visits, and $0.461$ for four or more visits (\Cref{fig:adjusted}). The 95\% confidence intervals were $[0.221,0.388]$, $[0.262,0.457]$, $[0.248,0.498]$, and $[0.350,0.572]$, respectively. All four comparisons with zero visits remained statistically significant after Holm adjustment across the ten pairwise comparisons.

None of the six pairwise comparisons among students who attended at different frequencies remained significant after Holm adjustment. The largest adjusted difference was between one visit and four or more visits ($0.156$ standard deviations); the unadjusted $p$-value was $0.025$ and the Holm-adjusted value was $0.153$. The adjusted results showed the same pattern: students in all four attendance groups differed from students with no recorded visits, while differences among attendance frequencies were not statistically significant after Holm adjustment.

Excluding smaller instructor--period groups produced very similar estimates. In the full sample, the estimated differences for one, two, three, and four or more visits were $0.305$, $0.359$, $0.373$, and $0.461$ standard deviations, respectively. Restricting the analysis to instructor--period groups with at least 20 students retained 6,255 students across 165 groups and yielded corresponding estimates of $0.312$, $0.367$, $0.384$, and $0.457$ standard deviations.

\subsection{Sensitivity analyses}

Higher mean grades among students who attended CMAT were also observed when the non-numeric course outcomes were handled differently. \Cref{fig:sensitivity} compares mean standardised grades under the main outcome, uniform imputation below the pass mark, and an analysis restricted to numeric final grades. In all three analyses, students with no recorded visits had a negative mean $Z$ score and each group of students who attended CMAT had a positive mean. However, mean performance did not increase consistently with attendance frequency across all three analyses. In the analysis restricted to numeric grades, for example, the means for 1, 2, 3, and 4+ visits were approximately $0.256$, $0.193$, $0.326$, and $0.200$, respectively. Across the sensitivity analyses, students who attended CMAT consistently had higher mean grades than students who did not, whereas the ordering of the attendance groups varied.
