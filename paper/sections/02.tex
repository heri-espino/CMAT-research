\section{Institutional context}

CMAT is the mathematics support centre at Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla, Mexico. It operates as a dedicated room with tables and whiteboards where mathematics professors are available outside regular classes. During the study period the centre was staffed from 08:00 to 17:50, Monday to Friday. Students were informed about the service by mathematics professors, could seek assistance from a professor other than their course instructor, and registered their attendance when entering the centre. Each registration is treated as one visit. The attendance records identify use of the centre but do not record visit duration, content or the staff member involved.

The available CMAT records cover 11 consecutive Spring and Autumn academic periods from Spring 2019 through Spring 2024. During at least part of this interval, an institutional first-semester participation requirement included three CMAT visits. Attendance was therefore not determined solely by voluntary help-seeking, and the present study does not interpret visit count as a treatment dose or evaluate the participation requirement itself.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Study population}

The unit of analysis was the student's first eligible observed attempt at MU during a period with CMAT attendance coverage. Administrative equivalency, revalidation and other records that did not correspond to an observed instructor--period grading context were excluded. Numeric final grades were retained together with the adverse non-numeric codes baja voluntaria (BV; voluntary withdrawal), retiro temporal (RT; temporary withdrawal) and baja acad\'emica (BA; academic withdrawal). The resulting study sample contained 6,627 students distributed across 190 instructor--period groups.

Visits were linked by student and academic period. A CMAT registration counted towards exposure whenever it occurred during the same academic period as the MU attempt, irrespective of the subject label recorded for that visit. This period-wide definition is intentional because CMAT is a shared mathematics-support environment rather than a course-specific tutoring programme.

\subsection{Attendance-frequency groups}

The broad benchmark divided students into no recorded attendance and one or more visits. The principal frequency analysis was then restricted to the 1,234 students with positive attendance.

The upper attendance category was selected before inspecting Paper 2.1 grade means, pass rates or pairwise outcome tests. We retained exact visit counts while they remained sufficiently represented across instructor--period groups for the planned pairwise comparisons. Exact counts of one through six visits contained 517, 245, 170, 96, 55 and 50 students, respectively. However, separating six visits from the upper tail reduced the weakest overlap between adjacent frequency groups from 26 to 15 instructor--period groups. We therefore used 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6+ visits as the primary grouping. The 6+ category contained 151 students across 72 instructor--period groups and 38 instructors. A more granular grouping of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7+ visits was retained as an exploratory sensitivity analysis.

This rule is intended to preserve frequency resolution without choosing the top-code from outcome significance. We do not describe 6+ as a universally optimal threshold; it is the highest-resolution grouping that maintained reasonable comparison support under the present design.

\subsection{Performance outcomes}

The primary continuous outcome was final MU performance standardised within instructor--period group. For student $i$ in group $c$,
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\bar{Y}_{c}}{s_c},
\end{equation}
where $Y_{ic}$ is the completed final-grade value and $\bar{Y}_{c}$ and $s_c$ are the corresponding instructor--period mean and sample standard deviation. This standardisation is substantive because MU instructors set and grade their own assessments; differences in raw-grade location and dispersion across instructors and periods are therefore not treated as student-performance differences.

Numeric grades were left unchanged. For the continuous primary outcome, BV, RT and BA were assigned values below the institutional pass mark of 7.5 using the distribution of observed numeric grades below 7.5 within the same instructor--period group. A Gaussian kernel-density estimate with SciPy's default Scott bandwidth was used when the within-group pool was non-degenerate, with draws restricted to $[0,7.5)$ and random seed 42. Degenerate pools used empirical resampling, and groups with no observed sub-pass numeric grade used a uniform fallback below 7.5. A numeric complete-case standardised outcome was retained as a sensitivity analysis.

The binary outcome PASS equalled one for a numeric final grade of at least 7.5 and zero for a numeric grade below 7.5 or an outcome of BV, RT or BA. Unlike the continuous primary outcome, PASS does not assign a latent numeric value to administrative outcomes.

\subsection{Academic-result composition}

To avoid treating all non-passing outcomes as substantively identical, we classified final records into four descriptive states: PASS, numeric grade below 7.5, BV/RT and BA. A second table retained five states by separating BV and RT.

We then examined the composition of outcomes conditional on non-PASS. The broad academic-management contrast compared any administrative outcome (BA/BV/RT) with a numeric grade below 7.5. Because the administrative codes need not reflect the same process, narrower contrasts compared BV/RT with numeric failure and, most specifically, BV with numeric failure after excluding RT and BA. RT versus numeric failure was retained as a diagnostic contrast. These comparisons describe the type of observed adverse outcome; they do not identify why a student received or selected an administrative outcome.

Administrative outcomes may occur before the end of the academic period and can therefore provide less opportunity to accumulate CMAT visits.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

The benchmark models compared 0 visits with 1+ visits using instructor--period fixed effects, degree-programme indicators and standard errors clustered at the instructor--period level. For the continuous outcome, coefficients are expressed in standard-deviation units. For PASS and the conditional academic-management outcomes, linear-probability models were used so that coefficients are interpretable as adjusted percentage-point differences.

The principal frequency analysis was restricted to CMAT users and included indicators for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6+ visits. We first tested the joint null of equal adjusted outcomes across all positive attendance-frequency groups. We then estimated all 15 pairwise contrasts and adjusted the resulting $p$-values within each outcome family using the Holm procedure. Adjacent contrasts were retained explicitly because they address whether successive visit counts are empirically distinguishable. Heatmaps display the adjusted pairwise effect estimates; colour represents effect magnitude and direction rather than statistical significance.

We additionally described the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of the continuous outcome across attendance-frequency groups and repeated these summaries using numeric grades only. The 1--6 and 7+ grouping was used as an exploratory tail sensitivity. No formal equivalence claim is made in this draft because a practical equivalence margin has not yet been finalised.
