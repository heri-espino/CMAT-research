\section{Institutional context}

CMAT is the mathematics support centre at Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla, Mexico. It operates as a dedicated room with tables and whiteboards where mathematics professors are available outside regular classes. During the study period the centre was staffed from 08:00 to 17:50, Monday to Friday. Students were informed about the service by mathematics professors, could seek assistance from a professor other than their course instructor, and registered their attendance when entering the centre. Each registration is treated as one visit. The attendance records identify use of the centre but do not record visit duration, content or the staff member involved.

The available CMAT records cover 11 consecutive Spring and Autumn academic periods from Spring 2019 through Spring 2024. During at least part of this interval, an institutional first-semester participation requirement allowed students to satisfy a PPA participation activity by completing three CMAT visits. The third recorded visit can therefore have a different behavioural meaning from an otherwise identical additional visit: for some students it may mark completion of an institutional participation requirement rather than a purely self-initiated decision to seek further mathematical help. Attendance was consequently not determined solely by voluntary help-seeking, and the present study does not interpret visit count as a treatment dose or evaluate the causal effect of the PPA requirement itself.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Study population}

The unit of analysis was the student's first eligible observed attempt at MU during a period with CMAT attendance coverage. Administrative equivalency, revalidation and other records that did not correspond to an observed instructor--period grading context were excluded. Numeric final grades were retained together with the adverse non-numeric codes baja voluntaria (BV; voluntary withdrawal), retiro temporal (RT; temporary withdrawal) and baja acad\'emica (BA; academic withdrawal). The resulting study sample contained 6,627 students distributed across 190 instructor--period groups.

Visits were linked by student and academic period. A CMAT registration counted towards exposure whenever it occurred during the same academic period as the MU attempt, irrespective of the subject label recorded for that visit. This period-wide definition is intentional because CMAT is a shared mathematics-support environment rather than a course-specific tutoring programme.

\subsection{Attendance-frequency groups}

The broad benchmark divided students into no recorded attendance and one or more visits. The principal frequency analysis was then restricted to the 1,234 students with positive attendance.

The upper attendance category was selected before inspecting grade means, pass rates or pairwise outcome tests. We retained exact visit counts while they remained sufficiently represented across instructor--period groups for the planned pairwise comparisons. Exact counts of one through six visits contained 517, 245, 170, 96, 55 and 50 students, respectively. However, separating six visits from the upper tail reduced the weakest overlap between adjacent frequency groups from 26 to 15 instructor--period groups. We therefore used 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6+ visits as the primary grouping. The 6+ category contained 151 students across 72 instructor--period groups and 38 instructors. A more granular grouping of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7+ visits was retained as an exploratory sensitivity analysis.

This rule is intended to preserve frequency resolution without choosing the top-code from outcome significance. We do not describe 6+ as a universally optimal threshold; it is the highest-resolution grouping that maintained reasonable comparison support under the present design.

\subsection{Performance outcomes}

The primary continuous outcome was final MU performance standardised within instructor--period group. For student $i$ in group $c$,
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\bar{Y}_{c}}{s_c},
\end{equation}
where $Y_{ic}$ is the completed final-grade value and $\bar{Y}_{c}$ and $s_c$ are the corresponding instructor--period mean and sample standard deviation. This standardisation is substantive because MU instructors set and grade their own assessments; differences in raw-grade location and dispersion across instructors and periods are therefore not treated as student-performance differences.

Numeric grades were left unchanged. For the continuous primary outcome, BV, RT and BA were assigned values below the institutional pass mark of 7.5 using the distribution of observed numeric grades below 7.5 within the same instructor--period group. A Gaussian kernel-density estimate with SciPy's default Scott bandwidth was used when the within-group pool was non-degenerate, with draws restricted to $[0,7.5)$ and random seed 42. Degenerate pools used empirical resampling, and groups with no observed sub-pass numeric grade used a uniform fallback below 7.5. A numeric complete-case standardised outcome was retained as a sensitivity analysis.

The binary outcome PASS equalled one for a numeric final grade of at least 7.5 and zero for a numeric grade below 7.5 or an outcome of BV, RT or BA. Unlike the continuous outcome, PASS requires no latent numeric value for administrative outcomes and therefore provides a direct robustness check against the possibility that the principal attendance contrast is generated by the imputation procedure.

\subsection{Academic-result composition}

To avoid treating all non-passing outcomes as substantively identical, we classified final records into four descriptive states: PASS, numeric grade below 7.5, BV/RT and BA. A second table retained five states by separating BV and RT.

We then examined the composition of outcomes conditional on non-PASS. The broad academic-management contrast compared any administrative outcome (BA/BV/RT) with a numeric grade below 7.5. Because the administrative codes need not reflect the same process, narrower contrasts compared BV/RT with numeric failure and, most specifically, BV with numeric failure after excluding RT and BA. RT versus numeric failure was retained as a diagnostic contrast. These comparisons describe the type of observed adverse outcome; they do not identify why a student received or selected an administrative outcome.

Administrative outcomes may occur before the end of the academic period and can therefore provide less opportunity to accumulate CMAT visits.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

The benchmark models compared 0 visits with 1+ visits using instructor--period fixed effects, degree-programme indicators and standard errors clustered at the instructor--period level. For the continuous outcome, coefficients are expressed in standard-deviation units. For PASS and the conditional academic-management outcomes, linear-probability models were used so that coefficients are interpretable as adjusted percentage-point differences.

The principal frequency analysis was restricted to CMAT users and included indicators for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6+ visits. We first tested the joint null of equal adjusted outcomes across all positive attendance-frequency groups. We then estimated all 15 pairwise contrasts and adjusted the resulting $p$-values within each outcome family using the Holm procedure. Adjacent contrasts were retained explicitly because they address whether successive visit counts are empirically distinguishable. Heatmaps display the adjusted pairwise effect estimates; colour represents effect magnitude and direction rather than statistical significance.

We additionally described the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of the continuous outcome across attendance-frequency groups and repeated these summaries using numeric grades only. The 1--6 and 7+ grouping was used as an exploratory tail sensitivity. For that specification we also display pairwise $p$-value dashboards with the unadjusted matrix on the left and the Holm-adjusted matrix on the right. The unadjusted matrix is reported only to show where local contrasts arise before family-wise multiplicity correction; inferential conclusions are based on the omnibus tests and Holm-adjusted family. Because the PPA participation incentive occurs at three visits, the dashboards visually mark the boundary between two and three visits, but this is not analysed as a regression-discontinuity or threshold-treatment design: assignment to the institutional requirement, its detailed historical implementation and other determinants of reaching three visits are not modelled as exogenous. We did not conduct a formal equivalence test because no substantively defensible equivalence margin had been specified independently of the observed estimates; failure to reject equality is therefore not interpreted as evidence of equivalence.

To isolate completed numeric failure from administrative outcomes, we defined numeric failure as a final numeric grade below 7.5 and fitted fixed-effect logistic models among records with numeric final grades. We estimated a zero-inclusive model and a user-only model, retaining instructor--period fixed effects, degree-programme indicators and instructor--period-clustered covariance. For each attendance group, adjusted failure probabilities were obtained by standardising fitted probabilities over the empirical covariate distribution; their standard errors and 95\% confidence intervals used the delta method with the cluster-robust covariance matrix. Pairwise odds ratios were Holm-adjusted within each analysis family.

These outcomes were interpreted as an explicit robustness hierarchy. PASS tests the broad attendance association in the full cohort without imputing a latent grade to BV, RT or BA; numeric failure then removes administrative outcomes altogether; the numeric complete-case density analysis examines distributional shape using only observed grades; and the imputed continuous distribution is used last to evaluate how numerical representation of administrative outcomes modifies that shape. Agreement across the earlier stages indicates that an attendance--outcome association is not mechanically created by imputation, whereas divergence between complete-case and imputed density results identifies the extent to which administrative outcomes contribute to the lower-tail structure.

Because instructors recur across academic periods, we repeated the benchmark and positive-frequency outcome models with covariance clustered by instructor while retaining instructor--period fixed effects and the original instructor--period standardisation of grades. This sensitivity changes only the level at which residual dependence is allowed; it does not redefine the outcome or attendance groups.

Finally, we used finite Gaussian mixtures to describe distributional shape rather than to classify students into latent types. Within each zero-inclusive attendance group, univariate mixtures with $K\in\{1,2,3\}$ components were fitted to the numeric complete-case standardised grade using multiple EM initialisations. For $K=2$, the candidate starts included empirical quartiles and the fixed pair $(-1.1,0.5)$ as additional starts rather than imposed component locations. We compared BIC and ICL, posterior entropy, mean maximum responsibility and Ashman's $D$, and used a parametric-bootstrap likelihood-ratio comparison of $K=1$ versus $K=2$ with 199 bootstrap replicates. Component composition was summarised using posterior responsibilities rather than hard assignment. The same procedure was repeated on the imputed continuous outcome as a sensitivity to the treatment of administrative outcomes. To distinguish multi-component structure from ordinary asymmetry, we additionally fitted a one-component skew-normal distribution to exactly the same observations and compared its log-likelihood, AIC and BIC with the one-Gaussian and two-Gaussian candidates. We then tested the skew-normal explanation directly in every attendance group using a parametric bootstrap in which samples were generated from the fitted skew-normal null, both models were refitted, and the observed BIC advantage of the two-Gaussian model was compared with the bootstrap distribution. As a complementary out-of-sample diagnostic, repeated five-fold cross-validation compared held-out log predictive density for the skew-normal and two-Gaussian specifications. Both diagnostics were applied to the numeric complete-case outcome and repeated for the imputed outcome; throughout, mixture components are interpreted as density approximations rather than identified student types.
