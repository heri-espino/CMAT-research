CMAT is a mathematics support centre that provides additional support outside students' regular mathematics teaching. Each recorded attendance event is treated as one visit. The records show whether and how often a student attended, but they do not measure the duration or content of a visit, the amount of independent study that followed, or the nature of the interaction with support staff. The number of visits is therefore interpreted as a measure of attendance frequency rather than as a direct measure of the amount of tutoring received.

During part of the study period, three CMAT visits were relevant to a first-semester participation requirement. This institutional context may have influenced attendance patterns, but the present study does not evaluate the requirement or treat three visits as a causal threshold.

The administrative records may contain CMAT visits from more than one academic period for the same student. Using cumulative attendance would therefore assign visits made before or after MU to the grade from a particular MU attempt. To avoid this mismatch, the analysis counts only visits recorded in the same academic period as the MU attempt being analysed.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Study sample}

The study sample contains one observation per student, corresponding to the first MU attempt that meets the inclusion criteria. Equivalencies, revalidations, and other administrative records that do not represent an observed course attempt are excluded. Numeric grades are retained together with the non-numeric outcomes BV, RT and BA, which are treated as non-passes as described below. The analysis is restricted to academic periods covered by the CMAT attendance records, because absence of a recorded visit can only be interpreted as non-attendance when the attendance system was in operation. Of 7,777 eligible first-MU attempts across all observed periods, 6,627 occur during periods with CMAT attendance coverage and form the study sample.

The 6,627 students are distributed across 190 instructor--period groups. Median group size is 30 students (IQR 24--48; range 10--88), with a mean of 34.9. In 176 groups there is at least one student who attended CMAT and at least one with no recorded visits. For the individual attendance-frequency comparisons, 152 groups include both zero- and one-visit students, 114 both zero- and two-visit students, 84 both zero- and three-visit students, and 102 both zero- and four-or-more-visit students. The adjusted estimates are therefore based largely on comparisons within instructor--period groups that contain both attendees and non-attendees.

\subsection{Attendance}

For each student, attendance is measured as the number of CMAT visits recorded during the same academic period as the MU attempt. The main analysis uses the five attendance groups shown in \Cref{eq:mu-visit-groups}:
\begin{equation}
V\in\{0,\;1,\;2,\;3,\;4+\}.
\label{eq:mu-visit-groups}
\end{equation}
This grouping distinguishes students with no recorded attendance from students who attended once, twice, three times, or at least four times without assuming a linear relationship between number of visits and academic performance. The four-or-more category is used because individual visit counts become increasingly sparse at higher levels of attendance.

\subsection{Course performance}

The primary outcome is the final MU grade standardised within instructor--period group. For student $i$ in group $c$,
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\bar{Y}_{c}}{s_c},
\label{eq:classroom-relative-performance}
\end{equation}
where $c$ contains students taught by the same instructor in MU during the same academic period. We refer to $Z$ as the standardised grade; positive values indicate performance above the instructor--period group mean and negative values indicate performance below it.

Standardising grades within instructor--period group does not imply that all differences between instructors or academic periods are measurement error. Raw grades may reflect differences in assessment, grading practice, cohort composition, or other contextual conditions. The purpose of the standardisation is to reduce the influence of differences in grade distributions across instructor--period groups when comparing attendance and performance. It does not address student-level self-selection into mathematics support, and the resulting $Z$ score should not be interpreted as a general measure of mathematical proficiency.

The non-numeric outcomes BV, RT and BA are treated as non-passes. For the continuous outcome, they are assigned values below the passing grade of 7.5 using the distribution of observed numeric failing grades within the same instructor--period group. Gaussian kernel density estimation is used when the within-group distribution is sufficient; empirical resampling or a uniform draw below 7.5 is used otherwise. Two sensitivity analyses are reported: a uniform below-pass imputation for all three non-numeric outcomes and an analysis restricted to students with numeric final grades.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

We first report mean standardised grades and 95\% confidence intervals across the five attendance groups. Because the Brown--Forsythe test indicates unequal variances, equality of group means is assessed using Welch's one-way ANOVA. Pairwise post hoc comparisons use the Games--Howell procedure, which accommodates unequal group sizes and variances. These analyses describe differences in academic performance across attendance groups; they do not establish that attendance at mathematics support caused those differences.
