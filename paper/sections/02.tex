CMAT is a mathematics support centre that provides additional support outside students' regular mathematics teaching. Each recorded attendance event is treated as one visit. The records show whether and how often a student attended, but they do not measure the duration or content of a visit, the amount of independent study that followed, or the nature of the interaction with support staff. The number of visits is therefore interpreted as a measure of attendance frequency rather than as a direct measure of the amount of tutoring received.

During part of the study period, three CMAT visits were linked to a first-semester participation requirement. We report this because it may have influenced attendance patterns, but the present paper does not evaluate the requirement and does not treat three visits as a discontinuity or causal threshold.

The administrative records may contain CMAT visits from more than one academic period for the same student. Using cumulative attendance would therefore assign visits made before or after MU to the grade from a particular MU attempt. To avoid this mismatch, the analysis counts only visits recorded in the same academic period as the MU attempt being analysed.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Study sample}

The study sample contains one observation per student, corresponding to the first MU attempt that meets the study inclusion criteria. Administrative records that do not represent an observed course attempt, such as equivalencies and revalidations, are excluded. Numeric grades and non-numeric course outcomes retained by the study outcome definition are included. The analysis is restricted to academic periods for which CMAT attendance records are available, because students with no recorded visits can only be classified as non-attenders when the attendance system covers the corresponding period. There are 7,777 eligible first-MU attempts across all observed periods, of which 6,627 occur during periods with CMAT attendance coverage and form the study sample.

The 6,627 students are distributed across 190 instructor--period classes. Class size has a median of 30 students (IQR 24--48), a mean of 34.9, and a range of 10--88. Of the 190 classes, 176 include both at least one student who attended CMAT and at least one student with no recorded visits. For the specific attendance-frequency comparisons, 152 classes contain both zero- and one-visit students, 114 contain both zero- and two-visit students, 84 contain both zero- and three-visit students, and 102 contain both zero- and four-or-more-visit students. Thus, the adjusted comparisons are based on substantial within-class variation in attendance, although fewer classes contribute to comparisons involving the smaller attendance groups.

\subsection{Attendance}

For each student, attendance is measured as the number of CMAT visits recorded during the same academic period as the MU attempt. The main analysis uses the five attendance groups shown in \Cref{eq:mu-visit-groups}:
\begin{equation}
V\in\{0,\;1,\;2,\;3,\;4+\}.
\label{eq:mu-visit-groups}
\end{equation}
This grouping distinguishes students with no recorded attendance from students who attended once, twice, three times, or at least four times without assuming a linear relationship between number of visits and academic performance. The four-or-more category is used because individual visit counts become increasingly sparse at higher levels of attendance.

\subsection{Course performance}

The primary outcome is the final MU grade standardised within class. For student $i$ in class $c$,
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\bar{Y}_{c}}{s_c},
\label{eq:classroom-relative-performance}
\end{equation}
where a class consists of students taught by the same instructor in the same course and academic period. Because the analysis includes only MU, this is effectively an instructor-by-period grouping. We refer to $Z$ as the within-class standardised grade: positive values indicate performance above the class mean and negative values performance below the class mean.

Standardising grades within class does not imply that all differences between instructors or academic periods are measurement error. Raw grades may reflect differences in assessment, grading practice, cohort composition, or other class-level conditions. The purpose of the standardisation is to prevent differences in class grade distributions from driving a pooled association between CMAT attendance and academic performance. It does not address student-level self-selection into mathematics support, and the resulting $Z$ score should not be interpreted as a general measure of mathematical proficiency.

Three non-numeric codes in the academic records (BV, RT and BA) are treated as non-passes. For the primary continuous outcome, these records are assigned values below the passing grade of 7.5 using the distribution of observed numeric failing grades within the same class. Gaussian kernel density estimation is used when the within-class distribution is sufficient; empirical resampling or a uniform draw below 7.5 is used as a fallback when it is not. We assess the sensitivity of the results in two additional ways: by using a uniform below-pass imputation for all adverse outcomes and by restricting the analysis to students with numeric final grades.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

We first report mean within-class standardised grades and 95\% confidence intervals across the five attendance groups. Because the Brown--Forsythe test indicates unequal variances, equality of group means is assessed using Welch's one-way ANOVA. Pairwise post hoc comparisons use the Games--Howell procedure, which accommodates unequal group sizes and variances. These analyses describe differences in academic performance across attendance groups; they do not establish that attendance at mathematics support caused those differences.
