CMAT is a mathematics support centre that provides additional support outside students' regular mathematics teaching. Each attendance record represents one visit. The usage data show how often a student attended, but not the duration or content of individual visits. Visit counts are therefore used as a measure of attendance frequency, not as a measure of the amount of tutoring received.

During part of the study period, three CMAT visits were relevant to a first-semester participation requirement. This institutional context may have influenced attendance patterns; the requirement itself is not evaluated in this study.

The administrative records can contain CMAT visits from more than one academic period for the same student. Using cumulative attendance could therefore assign visits made before or after MU to the grade from a particular MU attempt. We consequently counted only visits recorded in the same academic period as the MU attempt being analysed.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Study sample}

The study sample contained one observation per student, corresponding to the first MU attempt that met the inclusion criteria. Institutional equivalency, revalidation, and other administrative records that did not represent an observed course attempt were excluded. Numeric grades were retained together with the non-numeric outcomes BV, RT and BA, which were treated as non-passing outcomes as described below. The analysis was restricted to academic periods covered by the CMAT attendance records, because a student could be classified as having no recorded attendance only when the attendance system covered that period. Of 7,777 eligible first-MU attempts across all observed periods, 6,627 occurred during periods with CMAT attendance coverage and formed the study sample.

The 6,627 students were distributed across 190 instructor--period groups, defined by instructor and academic period. Median group size was 30 students (IQR 24--48; range 10--88), with a mean of 34.9. In 176 groups there was at least one student who attended CMAT and at least one with no recorded visits. For the individual attendance-frequency comparisons, 152 groups included both zero- and one-visit students, 114 both zero- and two-visit students, 84 both zero- and three-visit students, and 102 both zero- and four-or-more-visit students. Most adjusted comparisons were therefore based on instructor--period groups containing both students who attended CMAT and students with no recorded visits.

\subsection{Attendance}

For each student, attendance was measured as the number of CMAT visits recorded during the same academic period as the MU attempt. The main analysis distinguished five groups: no recorded visits, one visit, two visits, three visits, and four or more visits. This allowed attendance frequency to be examined without assuming a linear relationship between number of visits and academic performance. Visits above three were combined because individual counts became increasingly sparse.

\subsection{Course performance}

The primary outcome was the final MU grade standardised within instructor--period group. For student $i$ in group $c$,
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\bar{Y}_{c}}{s_c},
\label{eq:standardised-grade}
\end{equation}
where $c$ denotes an instructor--period group. We refer to $Z$ as the standardised grade; positive values indicate performance above the instructor--period group mean and negative values indicate performance below it.

The standardised grade expresses performance relative to students in the same instructor--period group, reducing the influence of differences in grade distributions across teaching contexts. It does not address student-level self-selection into mathematics support and should not be interpreted as a general measure of mathematical proficiency.

The non-numeric outcomes BV, RT and BA were treated as non-passing outcomes. For the continuous outcome, they were assigned values below the pass mark of 7.5 using observed numeric grades below the pass mark within the same instructor--period group. When those grades showed variation, values were drawn from a Gaussian kernel density estimate; empirical resampling was used when the observed grades below the pass mark were degenerate, and a uniform draw below 7.5 was used when no numeric grade below the pass mark was available in the group. Two sensitivity analyses were used: uniform imputation below the pass mark for all three non-numeric outcomes and an analysis restricted to students with numeric final grades.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

We first compared mean standardised grades across the five attendance groups, reporting 95\% confidence intervals. Group means were compared using Welch's one-way ANOVA, followed by Games--Howell post hoc comparisons; these procedures accommodate unequal variances and group sizes. All analyses were interpreted as observational associations rather than causal effects of mathematics support.
