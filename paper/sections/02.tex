CMAT is the mathematics support centre at Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla, Mexico, and operates as a dedicated room with tables and whiteboards where mathematics professors are available to advise students outside their regular classes. During the study period, the centre was staffed from 08:00 to 17:50, Monday to Friday. Students were informed about the service by their mathematics professors and could seek assistance from a professor other than the one teaching their course. On entering CMAT, students registered their attendance; each registration is treated as one visit in the present study. The attendance records cover 11 consecutive Spring and Autumn academic periods, from Spring 2019 through Spring 2024. They show how often a student attended, but not the duration, content, or staff member involved in individual visits. Visit counts are therefore used as a measure of attendance frequency, not as a measure of the amount of tutoring received.

During part of the study period, an institutional first-semester participation requirement included three CMAT visits, so attendance was not determined solely by students' decisions to seek support. The requirement itself is not evaluated here.

The administrative records can contain CMAT visits from more than one academic period for the same student. Using cumulative attendance could therefore assign visits made before or after MU to the grade from a particular MU attempt. We consequently counted only visits recorded in the same academic period as the MU attempt being analysed.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Study sample}

The study sample contained one observation per student, corresponding to the first MU attempt that met the inclusion criteria. Administrative records that did not represent an observed MU course attempt---including equivalency and revalidation records---were excluded. Numeric grades were retained together with three non-numeric administrative withdrawal codes: baja voluntaria (BV; voluntary withdrawal), retiro temporal (RT; temporary withdrawal), and baja acad\'emica (BA; academic withdrawal). These were treated analytically as non-passing outcomes as described below. The analysis was restricted to academic periods covered by the CMAT attendance records, because a student could be classified as having no recorded attendance only when the attendance system covered that period. Of 7,777 eligible first MU attempts across all observed periods, 6,627 occurred during periods with CMAT attendance coverage and formed the study sample.

The 6,627 students were distributed across 190 instructor--period groups, defined by instructor and academic period. Median group size was 30 students (IQR 24--48; range 10--88), with a mean of 34.9. In 176 groups there was at least one student who attended CMAT and at least one with no recorded visits. For the individual attendance-frequency comparisons, 152 groups included both zero- and one-visit students, 114 both zero- and two-visit students, 84 both zero- and three-visit students, and 102 both zero- and four-or-more-visit students. The amount of within-group overlap differed by attendance category, ranging from 84 instructor--period groups for the three-visit comparison with zero visits to 152 groups for the one-visit comparison.

\subsection{Attendance}

For each student, attendance was measured as the number of CMAT visits recorded during the same academic period as the MU attempt. The main analysis distinguished five groups: no recorded visits, one visit, two visits, three visits, and four or more visits. This allowed attendance frequency to be examined without assuming a linear relationship between number of visits and academic performance. Visits above three were combined because individual counts became increasingly sparse.

\subsection{Course performance}

The primary outcome was the final MU grade standardised within instructor--period group. For student $i$ in group $c$,
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\bar{Y}_{c}}{s_c},
\label{eq:standardised-grade}
\end{equation}
where $c$ denotes an instructor--period group. We refer to $Z$ as the standardised grade; positive values indicate performance above the instructor--period group mean and negative values indicate performance below it.

The standardised grade expresses each student's performance relative to others in the same instructor--period group, placing grades from different groups on a common within-group scale. It does not address student-level self-selection into mathematics support and should not be interpreted as a general measure of mathematical proficiency.

The three withdrawal outcomes BV, RT and BA were treated as non-passing outcomes. For the continuous outcome, they were assigned values below the pass mark of 7.5 using observed numeric grades below the pass mark within the same instructor--period group. When those grades showed variation, values were drawn from a Gaussian kernel density estimate using SciPy's default Scott bandwidth; draws were restricted to $[0,7.5)$ and generated with random seed 42. Empirical resampling was used when the observed grades below the pass mark were degenerate, and a uniform draw below 7.5 was used when no numeric grade below the pass mark was available in the group. Two sensitivity analyses were used: uniform imputation below the pass mark for all three non-numeric outcomes and an analysis restricted to students with numeric final grades.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

We first summarised mean standardised grades across the five attendance groups, reporting 95\% confidence intervals. Welch's one-way ANOVA and Games--Howell post hoc comparisons were retained as unadjusted secondary comparisons because they accommodate unequal variances and group sizes. Primary inferential comparisons were based on linear regression models with instructor--period fixed effects and degree-programme indicators, with standard errors clustered at the instructor--period level. The main model included separate indicators for 1, 2, 3, and 4 or more visits, with no recorded visits as the reference category. We calculated all ten pairwise comparisons among the five attendance groups and adjusted the corresponding $p$-values using the Holm procedure. This specification bases the adjusted comparisons on within-instructor--period variation while allowing statistical dependence among students in the same instructor--period group. All analyses were interpreted as observational associations rather than causal effects of mathematics support.
