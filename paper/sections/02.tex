\section{Institutional context}

CMAT is the mathematics support centre at Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla, Mexico. It operates as a dedicated room with tables and whiteboards where mathematics professors are available outside regular classes. During the study period the centre was staffed from 08:00 to 17:50, Monday to Friday. Students were informed about the service by mathematics professors, could seek assistance from a professor other than their course instructor, and registered their attendance when entering the centre. Each registration is treated as one visit. The attendance records identify use of the centre but do not record visit duration, content or the staff member involved.

The available CMAT records cover 11 consecutive Spring and Autumn academic periods from Spring 2019 through Spring 2024. During at least part of this interval, the university's first-semester PPA participation scheme allowed students to satisfy a participation activity by completing three CMAT visits. PPA is intended to encourage students to take part in activities offered by the university; CMAT attendance was therefore one institutionally incentivised route to participation rather than a purely spontaneous measure of mathematics help-seeking. The first, second and third visits can consequently have mixed behavioural meanings, because a student may be seeking mathematical support, completing a participation activity, or doing both. By contrast, a fourth or later CMAT visit is no longer required to complete the three-visit CMAT option itself, although later attendance remains selected and may still reflect academic need, motivation, prior preparation or other unobserved characteristics. The present study therefore treats three visits as an interpretive threshold, not as an exogenous treatment cutoff.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Study population}

The unit of analysis was the student's first eligible observed attempt at MU during a period with CMAT attendance coverage. Administrative equivalency, revalidation and other records that did not correspond to an observed instructor--period grading context were excluded. Numeric final grades were retained together with the adverse non-numeric codes baja voluntaria (BV; voluntary withdrawal), retiro temporal (RT; temporary withdrawal) and baja acad\'emica (BA; academic withdrawal). The resulting study sample contained 6,627 students distributed across 190 instructor--period groups.

Visits were linked by student and academic period. A CMAT registration counted towards exposure whenever it occurred during the same academic period as the MU attempt, irrespective of the subject label recorded for that visit. This period-wide definition is intentional because CMAT is a shared mathematics-support environment rather than a course-specific tutoring programme.

\subsection{Attendance-frequency groups}

The main reporting grid contains eight categories: 0, exact 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 visits, and 7+ visits. Their sample sizes are 5,393, 517, 245, 170, 96, 55, 50 and 101, respectively. This grouping is used throughout the principal descriptive figures, Table~1 and the main pairwise outcome analyses.

The eight categories are retained individually because they correspond to substantively different parts of the attendance distribution. Zero visits identifies students with no recorded CMAT use during the MU period. One through three visits lie within the range that could be explained, at least in part, by the three-visit PPA credit option. Four through six visits necessarily go beyond completion of that specific three-visit CMAT activity and therefore represent repeated use that is less directly attributable to the PPA threshold itself. The 7+ category isolates a high-frequency tail whose members returned substantially more often than the participation minimum.

The upper cut was chosen from both student counts and the instructor--period structure used for grade standardisation and fixed-effect adjustment. Exact six visits still contained 50 students distributed across 36 instructor--period groups, whereas exact seven, eight and nine visits contained only 25, 19 and 11 students across 23, 17 and 10 groups, respectively. Pooling seven or more visits yields 101 students and substantially more support for adjusted comparisons. By contrast, separating exact seven and pooling only 8+ would reduce the minimum overlap between adjacent attendance groups from 15 to 7 instructor--period groups, while an exact-eight/9+ specification would reduce it further to 4. We therefore retain exact counts through six visits and pool the remaining tail as 7+, balancing frequency resolution against the cross-classroom support required by the standardised and fixed-effect analyses.
\subsection{Performance outcomes}

The primary continuous outcome was final MU performance standardised within instructor--period group. For student $i$ in group $c$,
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\bar{Y}_{c}}{s_c},
\end{equation}
where $Y_{ic}$ is the completed final-grade value and $\bar{Y}_{c}$ and $s_c$ are the corresponding instructor--period mean and sample standard deviation. This standardisation is substantive because MU instructors set and grade their own assessments; differences in raw-grade location and dispersion across instructors and periods are therefore not treated as student-performance differences.

Numeric grades were left unchanged. For the continuous primary outcome, BV, RT and BA were assigned values below the institutional pass mark of 7.5 using the distribution of observed numeric grades below 7.5 within the same instructor--period group. A Gaussian kernel-density estimate with SciPy's default Scott bandwidth was used when the within-group pool was non-degenerate, with draws restricted to $[0,7.5)$ and random seed 42. Degenerate pools used empirical resampling, and groups with no observed sub-pass numeric grade used a uniform fallback below 7.5. A numeric complete-case standardised outcome was retained as a sensitivity analysis.

The binary outcome PASS equalled one for a numeric final grade of at least 7.5 and zero for a numeric grade below 7.5 or an outcome of BV, RT or BA. Unlike the continuous outcome, PASS requires no latent numeric value for administrative outcomes and therefore provides a direct robustness check against the possibility that the principal attendance contrast is generated by the imputation procedure.

\subsection{Academic-result composition}

To avoid treating all non-passing outcomes as substantively identical, we classified final records into four descriptive states: PASS, numeric grade below 7.5, BV/RT and BA. A second table retained five states by separating BV and RT.

We then examined the composition of outcomes conditional on non-PASS. The broad academic-management contrast compared any administrative outcome (BA/BV/RT) with a numeric grade below 7.5. Because the administrative codes need not reflect the same process, narrower contrasts compared BV/RT with numeric failure and, most specifically, BV with numeric failure after excluding RT and BA. RT versus numeric failure was retained as a diagnostic contrast. These comparisons describe the type of observed adverse outcome; they do not identify why a student received or selected an administrative outcome.

Administrative outcomes may occur before the end of the academic period and can therefore provide less opportunity to accumulate CMAT visits.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

We first estimated the broad benchmark comparing 0 visits with 1+ visits using instructor--period fixed effects, degree-programme indicators and standard errors clustered at the instructor--period level. Continuous-outcome coefficients are expressed in standard-deviation units. PASS and the conditional academic-management outcomes were analysed with linear-probability models so that coefficients can be interpreted directly as adjusted percentage-point differences.

The main frequency analysis then used all 6,627 students and the eight attendance indicators 0, exact 1--6 and 7+. For the continuous outcome, we tested the joint null of equal adjusted outcomes across all eight groups and estimated all 28 pairwise contrasts. The same full-group fixed-effect linear-probability model was used for multiplicity-aware PASS inference. Within each outcome family, pairwise $p$-values were adjusted with the Holm procedure. Adjacent comparisons were retained explicitly because they show where the descriptive profile changes from one attendance count to the next; the two-versus-three comparison is institutionally motivated by the PPA threshold, whereas any apparent changes at four-versus-five or six-versus-7+ are interpreted as exploratory features of the observed profile rather than pre-specified discontinuities.

For interpretability of the binary outcome, we additionally fitted a fixed-effect logistic model with the same attendance indicators, instructor--period fixed effects and degree-programme adjustment, and report pairwise odds-ratio point estimates. In the full eight-group logistic fit, the instructor--period-clustered covariance matrix was rank-deficient when clustering at the same level represented by the fixed effects. Logistic standard errors and $p$-values are therefore not used for inference. The PASS significance dashboard uses the stable fixed-effect linear-probability tests, while the odds-ratio panel is used only to show relative magnitude and direction.

The pairwise dashboards place all attendance frequencies in a common frame. Raw $p$-values are shown to identify where local evidence arises, and Holm-adjusted values are shown beside them for family-wise interpretation. A dashed boundary between two and three visits marks the institutional PPA threshold. This is not a regression-discontinuity analysis: assignment to the requirement, whether a particular visit was motivated by PPA, and other determinants of reaching three visits are not observed as exogenous variables.

We additionally examined the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of the continuous outcome and repeated the summaries using numeric grades only. The earlier pooled 6+ specification was retained as a support-based robustness check.

To isolate completed numeric failure from administrative outcomes, we defined numeric failure as an observed numeric final grade below 7.5 and fitted fixed-effect logistic models among students with numeric final grades. This analysis removes BV, RT and BA entirely and therefore tests whether the broad attendance pattern can be seen without assigning any latent numerical value to administrative outcomes. The numeric-failure robustness analysis retains the earlier pooled 6+ upper category and is not used to define the exact-six versus 7+ main result.

Distributional heterogeneity was examined in two complementary datasets. The numeric complete-case analysis uses only observed final grades and therefore asks whether the recorded numeric outcomes themselves require more than one distributional mode. The imputed analysis uses the completed continuous outcome after the pre-specified within-instructor-period imputation of BV, RT and BA and therefore asks how the full academic-outcome structure changes when administrative endings are represented on the same continuous scale.

For each attendance group, Gaussian mixtures with $K\in\{1,2,3\}$ components were fitted using multiple EM initialisations. Groups 0--5 were estimated from the original zero-inclusive fit, while exact six and 7+ were re-estimated separately rather than obtained by partitioning the old pooled 6+ model. For $K=2$, candidate starts included empirical quartiles and the fixed pair $(-1.1,0.5)$ as additional starting values rather than imposed component locations. We compared BIC and ICL, posterior entropy, mean maximum responsibility and Ashman's $D$, and used a parametric-bootstrap likelihood-ratio comparison of one versus two Gaussian components.

Because two Gaussian components can mimic a single asymmetric density, the principal shape check compared the two-Gaussian mixture directly with a one-component skew-normal distribution. Evidence was triangulated using AIC/BIC, a parametric bootstrap under the fitted skew-normal null and repeated five-fold cross-validation of held-out log predictive density. We report lower- and higher-mean component weights and locations for both the observed-grade and imputed analyses, but do not interpret those components as literal latent student types unless the shape diagnostics support a genuine two-component representation. Posterior state composition is used to determine whether the lower imputed component is enriched in numeric failure or administrative outcomes.

Because instructors recur across periods, we also retain an earlier sensitivity that clusters covariance by instructor while preserving instructor--period fixed effects and grade standardisation. That analysis uses the pooled 6+ tail and is treated as supplementary to the full eight-group results.

We did not conduct formal equivalence tests because no substantively defensible equivalence margin had been specified independently of the observed estimates; failure to reject a contrast is therefore not interpreted as evidence that two attendance groups are equivalent.
