\section{Institutional context}

The Centro de Aprendizaje de Matem\'aticas (CMAT) is a mathematics support centre at Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla, Mexico. Its working space includes tables and whiteboards, and mathematics professors provide assistance outside regular classes. The existing institutional description records weekday availability from 08:00 to 17:50 during the study period. A student could receive help from someone other than their regular course instructor; a centre entry was recorded as a visit, without a corresponding measure of duration, tutoring content or individual instructional intensity.

The administrative attendance series covers 11 consecutive spring and autumn academic periods from spring 2019 to spring 2024. Throughout the study window, PPA1 operated as a first-semester programme of social and cultural activities intended to help new students integrate into university life. Students could participate by attending sporting events, theatre or dance performances, or engaging with student organisations; completing three CMAT visits was one possible PPA1 activity. This option remained available throughout the 11 observed periods, according to institutional information provided to the study. For an eligible participant, the third CMAT visit completes this particular PPA1 activity, whereas a fourth visit exceeds its requirement. However, the MU records do not establish PPA1 eligibility or actual credit receipt for every included student, nor do they reveal why an individual visited CMAT or which alternative PPA1 activities were selected. The programme's integration purpose is distinct from mathematical help-seeking, and the visit count may therefore mix academic and non-academic motives. Neither continuity of the programme nor the three-visit requirement constitutes an exogenous treatment threshold; a written copy of the institutional programme rules should be retained for documentation.

\section{Data and methods}

\subsection{Population, attendance and study window}

We analyse each student's first eligible observed attempt at Matem\'aticas Universitarias (MU) during an academic period covered by CMAT records. Administrative equivalence, revalidation and other transactions without an eligible instructor--period grading context are excluded. The resulting sample has 6,627 students across 190 instructor--period contexts.

Attendance is counted at the student--period level, including all centre visits within the same academic period as MU, irrespective of the subject label recorded for a visit. Zero visits means no \emph{recorded} centre use, not absence of academic effort or need; a positive visit count measures entry frequency, not time spent receiving mathematical instruction.

\subsection{Frequency categories and the chronology of their selection}

The outcome-blind support audit originally selected exact 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 visits and a pooled $6+$ category for the principal comparison among users. This choice considered both student counts and overlap within the instructor--period groups required by the fixed-effect analysis. Distinguishing exact six from $7+$ reduced the minimum instructor--period overlap between adjacent attendance groups from 26 to 15. Thus, the pooled-$6+$ categories constitute the more defensible pre-outcome support specification rather than a threshold chosen to maximise significant contrasts.

For descriptive transparency and an exploratory finer-grained comparison, we additionally report the zero-inclusive grid 0, exact 1--6 and $7+$ visits. Counts for these eight levels are respectively 5,393, 517, 245, 170, 96, 55, 50 and 101. The finer top-code was adopted after the original support decision and is not presented as though it had been frozen before examining outcomes. We report adjusted comparisons both in the main among-user support-based analysis and in the zero-inclusive exploratory grid, with separately identified families of tests. For interpretation only, we overlay four institutional/descriptive bands: $0$ (5,393 students), $1$--$3$ (932), $4$--$6$ (201), and $7+$ (101). These bands highlight the PPA1 completion rule and the small upper tail, but they are not regrouped regression categories and no claim of four distinct adjusted effects follows from the display.

\subsection{Final academic outcomes}

The binary variable PASS equals one for a final numeric grade $\geq7.5$, including a grade of exactly 7.5, and equals zero for a numeric grade \emph{strictly} below 7.5 or for the recorded administrative states baja voluntaria (BV), retiro temporal (RT) and baja acad\'emica (BA). PASS uses no numerical imputation for the administrative states. Descriptive outcome figures distinguish numeric failure ($Y<7.5$) from administrative records without a numeric final grade (BV/RT/BA); the latter are not presumed to be voluntary decisions or equivalent academic situations.

The continuous outcome is standardised within the instructor--period grading context. Let $Y_{ic}$ denote the completed numeric final value for student $i$ in context $c$; then
\begin{equation}
Z_{ic}=\frac{Y_{ic}-\overline{Y}_c}{s_c},
\end{equation}
where $\overline{Y}_c$ and $s_c$ are the mean and sample standard deviation of the completed grades within that instructor--period. Numeric grades are unchanged. For this continuous specification, BV, RT and BA are first assigned values below 7.5 using the existing context-specific imputation rule based on observed sub-passing numeric grades (kernel-density sampling with a fixed seed and appropriate fallback rules). Such assignments allow a single standardised final-outcome variable but introduce a modelling assumption that is absent from PASS. A sensitivity restricted to observed numeric final grades is therefore interpreted as a distinct complete-case population rather than the same outcome without uncertainty.

\subsection{Adjusted comparisons and multiplicity}

The zero-versus-any-attendance benchmark is estimated with instructor--period fixed effects, degree-programme indicators and standard errors clustered by instructor--period. We report adjusted standardised-grade differences in SD units and pass-probability contrasts from a fixed-effect linear probability model in percentage points.

The primary attendance-frequency question concerns students with one or more recorded visits ($n=1,234$). Within this group, we fit categorical-frequency indicators alongside the same contextual and degree controls, first test the omnibus null of equal adjusted outcomes and then report pairwise Wald contrasts. The pooled-$6+$ grouping has six positive-frequency levels and therefore 15 pairs per outcome; the exploratory exact-six/$7+$ grouping has seven levels and 21 pairs per outcome. The separate zero-inclusive grid has eight levels and 28 pairs per outcome. The Holm procedure adjusts the resulting pairwise $p$-values \emph{within each explicitly stated outcome and specification family}. Contrasts between adjacent frequencies are reported to assess local resolution; a wider comparison between one visit and the high-frequency tail is distinct from evidence for a successive-visit gradient.

The continuous model uses ordinary least squares and the binary model a linear probability specification. Wald confidence intervals and hypothesis tests use the cluster-robust covariance; a sensitivity reclusters by instructor to allow correlation across academic periods involving the same instructor. The zero-versus-$1+$ benchmark is treated as conceptually distinct from frequency contrasts within users.

Absence of statistical significance does not demonstrate equivalence of attendance categories, and we do not report formal equivalence tests because an educationally justified equivalence margin was not fixed independently of the observed estimates. The analysis is observational and no coefficient is interpreted as a causal return to additional CMAT visits.
