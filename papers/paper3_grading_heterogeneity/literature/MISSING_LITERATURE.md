# Paper 3 — missing / targeted literature

Paper 3 currently has the largest substantive literature gap in the portfolio.

## Highest-priority search areas

- Higher-education studies of instructor/section grading heterogeneity within the same or comparable courses.
- Evidence on grading severity/leniency and comparability of raw course grades.
- Multilevel studies decomposing student-, classroom-, instructor- and period-level variation in grades.
- Assessment literature on standardizing or contextualizing grades for comparative analysis.
- Research on reliability/validity of course grades as indicators of achievement or learning.
- Studies explicitly separating differences in grading practice from differences in student composition.

## Mathematics-specific gaps

- Undergraduate mathematics studies comparing assessment distributions across instructors/sections.
- Calculus or first-year mathematics studies using instructor/section adjustment.
- Research on grading/assessment heterogeneity as a source of bias in mathematics-education observational studies.

## Target-journal search

Prioritize recent articles from:

- *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education*;
- *Studies in Educational Evaluation*;
- *International Journal of Research in Undergraduate Mathematics Education* where measurement/assessment is central.

## Search discipline

For every candidate source, record:

- unit of comparison (student, section, instructor, course, institution);
- whether student composition is adjusted;
- whether grades are raw, standardized, moderated, or modelled hierarchically;
- whether the goal is fairness, reliability, causal inference, prediction, or descriptive comparability;
- whether the paper supports our measurement choice or challenges it.

Do not treat generic standardized-test literature as directly equivalent to instructor-assigned university course grades.
