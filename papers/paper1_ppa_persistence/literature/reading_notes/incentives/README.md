# Paper 1 incentive / behavioural-intervention reading notes

This folder contains technical reading notes for sources whose main scientific contribution concerns incentives, incentive removal/expiration, explicit behavioural contingencies, or low-touch behavioural interventions relevant to interpreting the PPA-to-post-PPA change in context.

These are **not** evidence that PPA is an exogenous treatment in the CMAT data. Their role is to sharpen competing mechanisms and comparators: immediate response to incentives, persistence after removal, decay/crowd-out, heterogeneous responsiveness, and the limits of low-touch nudges.

## Current notes

- `gneezy_2011.md` — mechanism/review anchor on direct incentive effects, crowd-out, and post-removal behaviour.
- `angrist_2009.md` — randomized first-year college experiment combining academic services and financial incentives.
- `leuven_2010.md` — randomized financial-reward experiment with average null effects and heterogeneous response.
- `agnew_2021.md` — within-course incentive removal for online quizzes plus historical-cohort comparison.
- `oreopoulos_petronijevic_2019.md` — five-year RCT program of low-touch nudges/coaching with precise academic nulls.
- `blondeel_2023.md` — randomized nudge offer with null ITT-like group effects but positive post-treatment click-intensity associations.
- `damgaard_nielsen_2018.md` — broad review/typology of nudging in education and heterogeneous effects.

## Comparison dimensions to use across notes

For synthesis, compare each source on:

1. **Intervention class:** material incentive, contingent grade credit, nudge, coaching, information, reminder, etc.
2. **Target behaviour:** attendance, study effort, support use, quiz completion, grades, persistence.
3. **Assignment vs uptake:** randomized offer/exposure versus actual participation/clicking/use.
4. **Time structure:** introduction, active incentive period, removal/expiration, and follow-up.
5. **Outcome horizon:** immediate behaviour, end-of-course achievement, later persistence/achievement.
6. **Identification strength:** RCT/ITT, IV/LATE, quasi-experimental comparison, within-person change, correlational post-treatment analysis, review/theory.
7. **Heterogeneity:** ability, gender, socioeconomic background, baseline risk, engagement with the intervention.
8. **Post-incentive interpretation:** persistence, decay, crowd-out, or no evidence because follow-up is absent.
9. **CMAT transportability:** how close the intervention is to a first-year participation incentive tied to formal academic support.

## Paper 1 boundary

The strongest defensible use of this literature is comparative: it shows that incentives and nudges can alter participation, that response is often heterogeneous, that removal can change behaviour, and that persistence after an intervention is an empirical question. It does **not** justify estimating a causal PPA effect from the observational CMAT records.
