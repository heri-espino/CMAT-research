# Damgaard & Nielsen (2018) — *Nudging in Education*

## Citation

Damgaard, M. T., & Nielsen, H. S. (2018). Nudging in education. *Economics of Education Review, 64*, 313–342. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2018.03.008

## 60-second summary

This review maps behavioural interventions in education and emphasizes that nudges work unevenly across contexts and populations. The key lesson for Paper 1 is that behavioural mechanisms matter: interventions are more likely to help when they target a real behavioural barrier, and untargeted interventions are often weak or heterogeneous. The paper also explicitly distinguishes nudges from interventions that materially change economic incentives or impose requirements.

That distinction is important for CMAT/PPA. PPA should not casually be called a nudge if it materially changes incentives for participation. Damgaard & Nielsen are most useful as a **contrast/typology source** showing where low-touch behavioural interventions sit relative to incentive-linked policies.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** core behavioural-economics review / contrast source.
- **Used in:** literature review and Discussion.
- **Main job:** distinguish nudges from material incentives; motivate mechanism-specific and heterogeneous response.
- **CMAT connection:** helps prevent the PPA setting from being mislabeled as a generic nudge.

## Design and method

- **Study type:** structured review of education nudging interventions.
- **Scope:** preschool through higher education.
- **Studies identified:** `122` nudging studies; `57` had been published as working papers or journal articles since January 2016.
- **Inferential statistics:** no pooled meta-analytic effect is estimated.
- **Organizing dimensions:** active vs passive decision-making and changes/additions to the decision environment.
- **Intervention families:** defaults/framing, reminders/deadlines/goal setting, information/assistance, boosts, social comparison, extrinsic motivation, and brief psychological interventions.

## Main findings to remember

- Few interventions produce positive effects for everyone.
- Beneficial effects are often concentrated among people who actually face the behavioural barrier targeted by the intervention.
- Information/assistance and self-regulatory tools often help when they address a specific friction.
- Motivational nudges and extrinsic-motivation interventions show heterogeneous—and sometimes negative—effects.
- The authors stress theory of change, targeting, external validity, and scalability.

## Important definitional boundary

The review adopts the standard nudge idea of altering behaviour without forbidding options or **significantly changing economic incentives**. It explicitly excludes much of the literature on interventions designed to offset immediate costs with immediate benefits and interventions imposing mandatory requirements.

For Paper 1 this means:

> PPA can be compared with behavioural-intervention literature, but it should not automatically be labeled a nudge.

## What this paper lets us say

- Behavioural interventions in education are highly context- and mechanism-dependent.
- Targeting a specific behavioural barrier matters.
- Low-touch nudges should be distinguished from material incentive structures and requirements.
- Heterogeneity and external-validity concerns should be expected.

## What it does **not** let us say

- PPA is a nudge in the strict Thaler–Sunstein sense.
- Nudging evidence identifies what caused CMAT attendance.
- A null average effect means no subgroup responds.
