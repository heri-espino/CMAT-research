# Oreopoulos & Petronijevic (2019) — *The Remarkable Unresponsiveness of College Students to Nudging*

## Citation

Oreopoulos, P., & Petronijevic, U. (2019). *The Remarkable Unresponsiveness of College Students to Nudging and What We Can Learn from It*. IZA Discussion Paper No. 12460 / NBER Working Paper No. 26059.

## 60-second summary

This paper synthesizes a five-year experimental program testing scalable online, text-message, mindset, goal-setting, and coaching interventions among nearly 25,000 college students. The central result is a precise academic null: none of the interventions significantly improved grades or persistence, even among higher-risk students. Some coaching interventions improved intermediate outcomes such as study time, tutor/instructor contact, stress, and subjective support, but these changes were not large enough to generate detectable academic gains.

For Paper 1, this is an important contrast case: low-touch behavioural interventions can change proximal behaviours without necessarily changing academic outcomes, and a behavioural mechanism requiring sustained effort may be much harder to shift than a one-time administrative action.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** core null/contrast comparator.
- **Used in:** literature review and Discussion.
- **Main job:** show limits of low-touch nudging for sustained college behaviour and academic outcomes.
- **CMAT connection:** helps distinguish persistence in actual support use from exposure to reminders/messages and cautions against assuming that behavioural prompts automatically produce durable academic change.

## Design and sample

- **Setting:** first-year economics courses across three University of Toronto campuses.
- **Period:** five-year experimental program.
- **Total sample:** approximately `25,000` students.
- **Assignment:** randomized treatment/control within successive experiments.
- **Intervention families:** goal setting, mindset, online coaching, one-way text messaging, two-way text coaching, and face-to-face coaching.
- **Academic outcomes:** grades and persistence.
- **Intermediate outcomes:** study time, tutoring/instructor contact, stress, subjective support, mental health/experience measures.
- **Registry:** AEA RCT Registry `AEARCTR-0000810`.

## Key results

### Academic outcomes

- None of the evaluated interventions significantly improved academic outcomes in the pooled five-year evidence.
- The authors report that they can rule out treatment effects larger than roughly `0.07 SD` on academic outcomes.
- Precise null effects also appear among students at higher risk of poor performance and at the less-selective commuter-style satellite campuses.

### Intermediate behaviours

- Coaching-based interventions increased study time by roughly **2 hours per week on average**.
- Coaching also increased some forms of help-seeking/contact and improved subjective well-being/support measures.
- Students reported studying about **5–8 hours fewer per week than they planned**, and the interventions did not eliminate this effort gap.

## Identification / mechanism lesson

The interventions are randomized, so the treatment-offer effects are causally interpretable within each experiment. But the paper's mechanism analysis shows that moving an intermediate outcome is not sufficient if the change is too small to alter the final academic production process.

This is relevant to CMAT because later CMAT use is itself a behaviour of substantive interest; it should not be reduced to a proxy for eventual grades.

## What this paper lets us say

- Low-cost nudges aimed at sustained study behaviour can produce precise academic nulls.
- Intermediate behavioural/experience outcomes may move even when grades and persistence do not.
- One-time administrative nudges and sustained-effort behaviours are different intervention problems.
- Large samples and randomized designs are useful for distinguishing "no detectable effect" from underpowered ambiguity.

## What it does **not** let us say

- PPA or CMAT are equivalent to text-message nudging.
- Failure to change grades implies no behavioural response.
- Later CMAT persistence should necessarily improve grades.
- Observational persistence in CMAT has the same identification as these RCTs.
