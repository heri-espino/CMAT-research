# Kahu & Nelson (2018) — *Student engagement in the educational interface*

## Citation

Kahu, E. R., & Nelson, K. (2018). Student engagement in the educational interface: understanding the mechanisms of student success. *Higher Education Research & Development, 37*(1), 58–71. https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2017.1344197

## 60-second summary

This paper refines Kahu's earlier framework by proposing the **educational interface**: the psychosocial space where student characteristics and institutional practices meet. It highlights four candidate mechanisms — **self-efficacy, emotions, belonging and well-being** — through which that interaction may shape engagement and later success.

For CMAT, the framework is useful because PPA, first-year mathematics, and the availability of CMAT are institutional conditions encountered by students with different prior preparation, preferences and needs. Persistent later CMAT use could be interpreted within this framework as an observed behavioural trajectory, but the four mediators are not observed in the administrative data.

## What this paper lets us say

- The same institutional support may be experienced differently by different students.
- A first-year-only transition lens may miss ongoing interactions between student and institution.
- Future CMAT surveys should consider self-efficacy, belonging, affect and perceived accessibility/familiarity if we want to test mechanisms.

## What it does **not** let us say

- CMAT visits measure any of the four mediators.
- PPA caused self-efficacy, belonging or habit formation.
- The proposed mediation structure has been statistically identified in our data.
