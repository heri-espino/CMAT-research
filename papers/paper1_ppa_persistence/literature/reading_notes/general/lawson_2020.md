# Lawson, Grove & Croft (2020) — *The evolution of mathematics support: a literature review*

## Citation

Lawson, D., Grove, M., & Croft, T. (2020). The evolution of mathematics support: a literature review. *International Journal of Mathematical Education in Science and Technology, 51*(8), 1224–1254. https://doi.org/10.1080/0020739X.2019.1662120

## 60-second summary

This paper explains how organised mathematics support became a durable part of higher education, especially in the UK, Ireland and Australia. It reviews the field through four broad questions: who uses support and who does not, who provides it, where it sits institutionally, and how its effectiveness is evaluated.

For Paper 1, its main value is **field definition and historical context**. It helps us explain that CMAT is not an unusual intervention invented for this study; it belongs to an established family of institutional support services offered in addition to ordinary teaching.

## Role in Paper 1

- **Priority:** core context.
- **Used in:** Introduction / institutional framing / Discussion.
- **Main job:** define mathematics support and explain its evolution from remedial provision toward broad institutional academic support.
- **Paper 1 connection:** CMAT can be positioned as organised mathematics support whose use is student-initiated within an institutional context.

## Design and method

- **Study type:** literature review / field synthesis.
- **Coverage:** mathematics-support scholarship mainly since 2000, with selected earlier foundational work.
- **Sources:** English-language mathematics-education and broader higher-education journals plus selected grey literature; conference papers were not systematically reviewed.
- **Synthesis:** narrative/thematic review.
- **Meta-analysis:** none.
- **Primary inferential statistics:** **not applicable**. The paper does not estimate a pooled β, R², p-value or average treatment effect.

The authors describe their journal review as systematic, while explicitly noting that the grey-literature review was less systematic.

### Useful historical numbers reported in the review

These are contextual findings from prior reports cited by Lawson et al., not new treatment estimates from this paper:

- one historical survey reported `83%` of academics expressing substantial concern about students' algebraic manipulation;
- A-level mathematics entries in the cited UK context fell from `66,247` in 2001 to `53,940` in 2002, nearly a 20% decline.

These numbers are **not central Paper 1 results** and should only be used if we need historical UK context.

## Short quotation worth remembering

> “in addition to their regular programmes of teaching”

This concise phrase captures the field definition that makes CMAT recognisable as mathematics support.

## What this paper lets us say

- Mathematics support is an established higher-education service category.
- Student use is typically voluntary/self-initiated, which creates evaluation challenges.
- The field has expanded beyond strictly remedial support.

## What it does **not** let us say

- CMAT causes higher performance or persistence.
- A particular number of visits is optimal.
- The literature review provides a pooled causal effect.
